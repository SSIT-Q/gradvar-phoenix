# Deviation 67 (draft): Gate 2 (e) after Deviation 65: what pauses the campaign, what is handled per job, one re-run

**Status: draft, not adopted.** Drafted 11 Nov 2026, between 07:29 and 10:55 UTC, on branch `dev67-gate2e-scope`, cut from `main` at
a8136f0068711c43312918ffe9e8221f8a8bdc22 (pre-registration v0.18.0). This branch adds this file only; it does not edit the pre-registration, a job
list, a run bundle or any code. Nothing is armed or dispatched. The number 67 is the one the independent review assigned (its Section 8.5,
item 11) and is provisional until adoption. This Deviation changes a pause clause. It is therefore not adopted under delegation: it needs the PI's
own signature, in his words, with the date and time.

**Scope.** What pauses the campaign under Gate 2 (e), and one re-run of a job whose points cannot be read. It rests on draft Deviation 65
(`docs/deviations_drafts/65_execution_time_calibration_2026-11-10.md` on branch `dev65-execution-calibration`): the execution-time calibration
E(J), the bracket B(J) and the classification of each measured point are defined there and are not repeated here. No cut (Deviations 22, 26, 53),
hypothesis, test threshold, list, point, seed, prediction model or setting changes. No budget line or ledger line changes: a re-run spends minutes
of the list's existing lines and is refused when they would be exceeded.

**Why.** Draft Deviation 65 makes Gate 2 (e) read the readout errors in force when each job ran, so the clause can fail. The review then counted how
often it would: on the committed record the pause condition as Deviations 49 and 52 write it is met on 9 of 13 pairs of consecutive committed sets
for the n100 rung, and most of that is the 1.5× ratio firing on shot noise (review, Section 4 item 3 and Section 5.1). A clause that pauses the
campaign on most run days, each pause needing a signed deviation, cannot stand as written next to Deviation 65. The review set out three options
(its Section 5.2). The PI chose between them (Section 4 below).

**Sources.** The independent review of drafts 64, 65 and 66, `docs/reviews/review_dev64_dev65_dev66_2026-11-10.md` (verdict GO_WITH_CORRECTIONS; its
Sections 4, 5 and 8.5). That file was not on `main` at a8136f0 when this draft was written; the row below cites it by that path, so it has to be
committed before the row is adopted. Pre-registration v0.18.0 on `main` (`docs/artifacts/paper1_preregistration.md`: Section 5, Gate 2 (e) and the
pause rule; Deviations 49 and 52). Draft Deviation 65 as revised on 11 Nov 2026 and draft Deviation 66 (the pause record of 10 Nov). The lead's
instructions of 11 Nov 2026. The report of the QPU-free trial of 11 Nov
(`docs/deviations_drafts/65_support/trial_properties_history_20261111T041956Z.md` on branch `trial-properties-history-run-42958459321`). The
author of this draft was barred from the 10 Nov measured data and opened none of it.

## 4. The decision, as it was made

1. **The PI's choice.** Relayed by M. Owais on 10 Nov 2026, between 19:41 and 19:54 UTC: option (iii) unless its cost is high, otherwise option
   (ii). This is the form in which the choice reached this track, through the lead. The PI's own words are not in the record this track holds;
   they go into the two open fields of the row when they are supplied.
2. **The threshold.** Fixed by the lead before any cost estimate was seen. The review reports it as two tests (its Section 5.3 (c)): the expected
   cost of the re-runs against 10.2 min, one third of the day-3 list's modelled 30.6 min, and the worst case against the ledger lines that fund
   day 3. The lead's own statement of the threshold is not in this track's record either; the lead should confirm that these are its terms.
3. **The figures that met it** (all from the review's Section 5.3 (b) and (c); none was recomputed here except where Section 8 says so).
   Expected cost of the rule that re-runs not-evaluable jobs only: 5.8 to 7.3 min (3.0 to 7.5 s of overhead per job), up to 9.5 min if the three
   pairs in which no value changed are left out; under 10.2 min. If flagged jobs were re-run as well: 14.1 to 18.4 min, over the line, which is why
   a flag never triggers a re-run. Worst case, every job of day 3 re-run once: 61.2, 71.7 or 80.1 min (3.0, 5.5 or 7.5 s per job) against 82.5 min
   of lines (the 65-minute dial line, the 8.0-minute reference item, the 9.5-minute top-up); inside them in each case. With the Deviation 63
   pairs' list added, at day 2's overhead: 89.6 min, 7.1 min over the lines. The line condition written into part (iii) of the row caps that case:
   a re-run that would leave the lines is refused.
4. **How the lead applied it.** On 11 Nov 2026 the lead first read the worst case as exceeding the lines at the overhead measured on 10 Nov and
   instructed option (ii). He corrected this the same day: the worst case is inside the lines at every overhead, and only the case with the pairs
   at day 2's overhead exceeds them. The threshold is met and option (iii) follows, as the review concludes. It is recorded here because the choice
   between (ii) and (iii) turned on that reading.
5. **What is weak in it, stated plainly.** The review's sentence: "The margin is real on the point estimate and not robust to the uncertainty of
   13 pairs." The rate behind the expected cost is 2 of 13 per rung, with a 95 percent interval of 4 to 42 percent; the expected cost reaches
   10.2 min at a rate of about 0.27 (3.0 s per job) or 0.22 (7.5 s). The cost is also lumpy: two reference jobs hold 72 percent of the list's
   minutes. And since the trial of 11 Nov the 13 pairs are known to be pairs of sets the snapshot workflow happened to commit (on 10 Nov, three of
   the six sets IBM stamped), so a rate per pair is not a rate per run day. What bounds the cost is therefore not the expectation but the line
   condition of part (iii), which holds whatever the rate is.
6. **The review's condition.** Option (iii) is dispatched only if its code has been independently reviewed before dispatch. The review's words:
   "(iii) is the better design only if its code is reviewed before dispatch. If it is not, dispatch under (ii); a re-run improvised on the day
   would be worse than none." If the review of the code is not done in time, the row for option (ii) is the review's too (its Section 5.3 (d),
   last paragraph but one): the same row without part (iii) and without the sentence "A pause under (i) stops every further list but not this one
   re-run"; part (iv) replaced by "No job is re-run under this Deviation; whether a point that is not evaluable is measured again is a budget
   decision recorded as its own deviation."; the title ending "... what is handled per job".

## 8. Open points

1. The PI's words, date and time (the row's open fields), and the lead's confirmation of the threshold's terms (Section 4, items 1 and 2).
2. `docs/reviews/review_dev64_dev65_dev66_2026-11-10.md` must be on `main` before a row that cites it is adopted.
3. "The same UTC day as the first run". The review's rule (its Section 5.3 (a)) compares the clock with the day the job was created; the row says
   "the first run". A job created before midnight UTC and run after it (day 2's flagged job was created 20 Sep 18:58Z and ran 21 Sep 00:05Z) has
   two candidate days. The code takes the day of creation. The PI or the reviewer should say which is meant.
4. "13 pairs of consecutive committed IBM updates" in the row. After the trial these are pairs of committed sets: the snapshots do not catch every
   set IBM stamps, and a stamp is not always a change of values (draft 65, Section 4.2 and Section 10 item 8). The row's words are left as the
   review wrote them. If a sentence is wanted, this track proposes, after "values of the later; n100 rung;": "the committed sets are those the
   snapshot workflow caught, three of the six IBM stamped on 10 Nov, so the counts are counts over what was committed". Not applied.
5. A flagged point without a dispatch-time row. The 10 percent line of Deviation 65 (ii) needs both rows. When the package holds none for a
   point, the code leaves the point without a reading, and phase 2 of the retrieval then refuses and names it (the same when the two rows are not
   on one estimator).
   Whether such a point is "not evaluable on calibration grounds" (and so triggers a re-run) or is read with its flag is not decided by either
   row.
6. Order on the day. The re-run decision is computed between the two retrieval phases, before any measured value exists in the repository. The
   reset-error half of the pause condition reads measured probes, so it can only be evaluated after phase 2. A re-run can therefore be
   dispatched before a pause on the reset error is known. The row allows it ("A pause under (i) stops every further list but not this one
   re-run"); it is stated here so that nobody is surprised by it.
7. "Not evaluable on calibration grounds" includes, for a point read by a dial comparator, a cone qubit below the coherence floor (Deviation 64
   (4) as corrected). No code on `dev65-implementation` sets that ground; it belongs to the Deviation 64 implementation.
