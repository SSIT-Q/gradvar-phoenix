  A<sub>ℓ</sub> = Σ w over the diagonal strings and B<sub>ℓ</sub> = Σ w μ<sub>P</sub>, with μ<sub>P</sub> = Π<sub>q∈P</sub> μ<sub>q</sub> and

  Deviation 46 comparators (derivation and validation: `docs/PAULIPROP.md`, section 'Deviation 60').

### Item 6. The H6 control without a resolvably positive dephasing variance (found by the Deviation 63 track)

- **Before.** H6 is refuted if "the reset dial's k = L variance at n = 60, L = 8 does not exceed the dephasing dial's by the pre-drawn
  factor within the combined interval"; the analysis reads the clause as the paired-bootstrap ratio against the pre-drawn factor F, within
  the combined interval (`_ratio_test`) and with its lower limit above 1. The dephasing dial's predicted k = L variance is below one
  draw's shot floor (1.5551×10<sup>−5</sup> ± 4.0×10<sup>−6</sup> at 2 s.e. on the Deviation 62 placement, against 1/(2 × 4096) =
  1.22×10<sup>−4</sup>), so its floor-subtracted estimate is often at or below zero. `paired_ratio` then returned NaN, the 'above 1'
  flag was False and H6 failed; with a positive estimate whose interval reaches zero, the bootstrap dropped the resamples with a
  non-positive denominator, so the ratio's interval was conditional on them.
- **Rule (part (6), stated before the day-3 data).** When the lower end of the dephasing point's 95 % interval (the point table's, as in
  the floor test) is at or below zero, or the paired ratio has no positive denominator, no ratio is formed. The dephasing variance is read
  as an upper bound d<sub>hi</sub>, the upper end of that interval, as Deviation 40 reads the k = 1 rows; every ratio consistent with the two
  intervals is then at least r<sub>lo</sub>/d<sub>hi</sub> (r<sub>lo</sub> the lower end of the reset point's interval) and has no upper
  limit. The analysis's two conditions on that interval: 'above 1' becomes r<sub>lo</sub> > max(d<sub>hi</sub>, 0); 'the pre-drawn factor
  within the combined interval' becomes r<sub>lo</sub> ≤ F·e<sup>1.96s</sup>·d<sub>hi</sub>, the measured lower limit widened by the
  prediction's 1.96σ on the log scale as in `_ratio_test` (s the relative σ of F). The clause holds when both hold and fails otherwise;
  without a pre-drawn factor 'above 1' decides, as before. Both comparisons are products; no ratio with a non-positive denominator enters.
  A resolvably positive dephasing variance keeps the ratio test unchanged. When the dephasing interval lies entirely at or below zero
  (d<sub>hi</sub> ≤ 0), it excludes every admissible variance, a failure of the estimator's interval rather than evidence against F: the
  factor condition is then reported 'factor not tested' (`factor_tested` False in the control entry) and r<sub>lo</sub> > 0 decides; the
  H6 note records it (the checkpoint review's addendum 2, decided before data).
- **Why this reading.** It is the existing test in the limit where the dephasing interval reaches zero and the ratio interval's upper end
  goes to infinity, so only the case the ratio cannot decide changes. The part the data resolve at the design, the reset dial far above
  the dephasing dial, is still tested, and the factor can still be missed from above, when the dephasing variance is resolvably below its
  own prediction (0 < d<sub>hi</sub> < r<sub>lo</sub>/(F·e<sup>1.96s</sup>)).
- **Expected outcome** (`python scripts/h6_control_bounds_sim.py 400`: 400 simulated days at the predicted values of the Deviation 62
  placement, F = 627; M = 100; Student-t draws with 5 degrees of freedom, kurtosis 9, near Deviation 17's 8.4; floors known). The dephasing
  estimate is at or below zero on 24.5 % of days and its interval reaches zero on 91.5 %; it lies entirely at or below zero on 0.75 %. The
  clause holds on 97.0 % (98.9 % of the bound-rule days; 76 % of the others, where a resolvably positive dephasing estimate is an upward
  fluctuation and its ratio falls short of F) and H6 passes on 96.3 %; the previous code passed the clause on 73.5 % and H6 on 72.8 %, and
  put NaN in the control entry. The checkpoint review's independent simulation (1,000 days, before the d<sub>hi</sub> ≤ 0 addition) gave
  95.3 % against 73.9 %.
- **Code and tests.** `dial_hypotheses.H6_CONTROL_BOUNDS` and `_control_bounds_test`; the control entry records `rule` (`ratio` or
  `bounds`), both bounds and the factor's upper limit, and the H6 note says when the bounds decided. Three tests in
  `tests/test_dial_placement.py` (Section 8).

## 7. Considered and not adopted

- **Rule (b) for ℓ = 4**, a statistical flag (the statistic's lower limit against mean(sv)/K). With the shot term subtracted, the
  ideal-model ℓ = 4 point would be detected about two times in three (z ≈ 2.2–2.6), so the flag would call it an upper bound only about a
  third of the time and the label would depend on the data. The note excludes combining (b) with "expected to stay an upper bound".
- **√(MSD + shot²) against the statistic as Section 3b writes it.** The shot term depends on the measured per-mask values, so the
  comparator would have to be completed with measured quantities, and the Section 3b 'Analysis' Pairing bullet subtracts it. That statistic is reported
  beside (`rms_with_shot`).
- **A model-error term in `rms_l2_sigma`.** Not adopted for H5 / H6, so not added here (the note's item 1).
- **Keeping the interval comparison for the one-sided ℓ = 4 test** (the review's alternative for M2): not taken; the paired bootstrap is
  the test H7 names.
- **A mask replicate of the whole y<sub>d</sub>** (the review's M3 text read literally): centred on mean(diff)², so it adds the
  subtracted terms back (Section 10, M3).
- **Drawing the missing p = 0.5 rows on this branch.** The placement may change before the day; the script and the command are committed
  and the draw is left to the lead (item 5).

- **Deciding the H6 control by r<sub>lo</sub> > F·d<sub>hi</sub>** (the reset variance's lower bound above the pre-drawn factor times
  the dephasing upper bound, as the pass condition). At the predicted values F·d<sub>hi</sub> is about 3.3×10<sup>−2</sup> (median
  d<sub>hi</sub> 5.2×10<sup>−5</sup>) against r<sub>lo</sub> about 6.0×10<sup>−3</sup>, so the clause would hold on 2.5 % of simulated days and
  H6 would be refuted at its own prediction: it asks the design to bound the dephasing variance below r<sub>lo</sub>/F ≈ 1×10<sup>−5</sup>,
  under its predicted value, which 100 draws at 4096 shots per shift circuit cannot (the estimate's standard error is about
  2×10<sup>−5</sup>). Under the analysis's two-sided reading, r<sub>lo</sub> > F·e<sup>1.96s</sup>·d<sub>hi</sub> is the miss condition.
- **The H6 control not evaluable when the dephasing variance is unresolved.** The registered clause does not exempt an unresolved
  reference, and at the design's predicted values the sub-test would then almost never be evaluated.
