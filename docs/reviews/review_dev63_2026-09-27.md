### 2.4 The two truncation pairs

Ideal-model illustrations were reproduced exactly with the independent chain (Appendix A1), and are identical on the 23 Sep and 27 Sep
n60 rungs to the digits shown.

**(a) The reset dial at p = 0.25.** Its "above day 3's p = 0.5 RMS(2)" clause discriminates between two predictions. The dial law
predicts 0.130 against 0.057 (ratio 2.27). H7's own 2-design form, c^(ℓ/2) × std(C_mix) (Deviation 60 part (4)), predicts
0.583 × 0.0605 = 0.035 against 0.333 × 0.138 = 0.046 (ratio 0.77, the reverse order). The draft does not state the second prediction
(S2).

Power of the ordering test (provisional): z ≈ 7 with Gaussian per-draw differences, and ≈ 3.6 at a per-draw kurtosis of 9; the
one-sided 95 percent criterion is z > 1.645. So a two-sample design is adequate. At p = 0.25 the ℓ = 2 statistic tests the law's full
MSD, which the |0⟩-start term dominates (ideal: E[C²] 0.0076, 2B₂ 0.0200, A₂ 0.0293, MSD 0.0169). A single ℓ estimates no e-folding
depth.

**(b) The dephasing dial at p = 0.5.** For any unital dial at uniform angles, B_ℓ = 0 and MSD(ℓ) = E[C²] + A_ℓ is an identity
(reproduced: B₂ = 0, A₂ = 0.070312 = 9/128, RMS(2) = 0.265207). The pair therefore tests the realisation: no memory of |0⟩ on hardware,
and the engine's A₂ for the 2-layer circuit. It does not test a live alternative (S3). Its margin (0.208, ideal) is several times the
expected interval, so it will discriminate an R1-like from an R2-like pattern decisively. Because its refutation reads
RMS_dephase(2) − RMS_reset(2), a miss on H7 moves (b) by the same amount (S3).

**Shared design.** The geometry, estimator, statistic and interval follow the booked pair and Deviation 60. The fresh seed block
makes the between-list comparisons two-sample. That is sound, but it departs from Section 3b's pooling default without a stated reason
(S4).

## 6. Must-fix items

**M1. The E[C_mix] rule (R3 (ii)) is miscalibrated for the masks the runner uses.**

*Found.* `mean_cmix_check` compares the bootstrap interval of the measured mean with F = (a_i t_i + b_i)(a_j t_j + b_j),
t_q = p(1 − 2ε_q). That is the expected value for masks drawn fresh per circuit (Section 3b 'Mask'). `build_probes` (Deviation 48
packing) instead draws K = 256 masks per point, mask m from (seed + 1 + m, MASK_STREAM), and uses them for both shift circuits and all
100 draws. The lottery's sampling error is therefore common to every draw and not in the interval.

In the ideal model E_θ[C_m] = r_i^m r_j^m for mask m, where r is the last-layer reset indicator of each observable qubit. The expected
mean is therefore F̂ = (1/K) Σ_m Π_{q∈{i,j}} (a_q r_q^m (1 − 2ε_q) + b_q). On Deviation 62's day-3 list the realised fractions give
F̂ − F from −0.0117 to +0.0117 (Appendix A2). The family-wise half-widths are about 0.0105 at p = 0.25 and 0.025 at p = 0.5.

With the channel exactly as modelled, the rule as drafted fires on at least one of the six points with probability about 0.89
(provisional). The n40 and n100 points alone give about 0.58 and 0.59. With F̂ the rate is about 0.048. R3 overrides R1, so this rule alone
would make R3 the likely headline.

*Fix.*

1. Define the reference value as F̂ from the realised masks, in the row, in Section 4.2 and in `mean_cmix_check`. The masks are
   reconstructed from the logged mask seeds with `gradvar.hardware.mask_lottery`; the shift circuits share them. The docstring's "p²
   exact" must say "for fresh masks".
2. Fix the family in advance: the six reset k = L points of the day-3 list (p = 0.25 at L = 8 on the n40, n60 and n100 rungs and at
   L = 12 on n60; p = 0.5 at L = 8 and 12 on n60). Bonferroni m = 6; a point that cannot be evaluated counts as not missing, and m
   stays 6.
3. Take ε_q from day 3's characterisation probes (`reset_error_prep1` with `readout_ref_prep0/1`, n60 patch only) and use 0 on the n40
   and n100 rungs, saying why (the 2e-5 initialisation median bounds ε near 1e-4). Pass it from the analysis: no caller supplies `eps`
   now.
4. Add a test that plants a mask set with p̂_both ≠ p² and checks that the rule does not fire at F̂ and does fire under a planted p
   error.

**M2. R2 cannot occur without the unital control failing, so it cannot be read as "added noise only".**

*Found.* The dephasing dial is the delay-matched circuit with exact virtual-Z frame changes added. Both circuits have the same 400 ns
slot per layer: the reference carries explicit delays on every qubit, and the dial carries them on the masked qubits while the
per-layer barrier makes the others idle. In the built dial circuits every delay is preceded by rz(π), and nothing else differs. Adding a Pauli channel can only shrink the gradient's uniform-angle second moments (each path weight is multiplied by factors
≤ 1). So in any unital (Pauli) model the dephasing dial's k = L variance cannot exceed the delay-matched p = 0 k = L variance at n = 60,
L = 8.

On Deviation 62's placement that ceiling is 2.65e-4 (pre-drawn), against the reset dial's Deviation 33 floor of 7.54e-3 (a factor 28)
and the dephasing dial's own prediction of 1.56e-5. R2 needs the reset dial's k = L variance not to be resolvably above the dephasing
dial's while the reset floor clause holds. At M = 100, with the variance interval of Gate 1b (e) (hi/lo about 3), that needs a
dephasing variance of about 1.9e-3 or more: about 7 times the unital ceiling and about 120 times its prediction (provisional). R2's only
realisations are therefore a unital control that is not unital as built, or a pipeline fault. The draft's label would report either as
physics.

*Fix.* Choose one of:

- **(a)** Drop R2, leaving R1, R3 and UNRESOLVED. Part (1) then states that, within the model, Question 2's answer is fixed by the
  Deviation 33 floor against the unital ceiling, and the hardware test is whether the pre-drawn separation appears.
- **(b)** Keep R2, and add a unital-ceiling check. If the dephasing dial's floor-subtracted k = L variance is resolvably above the
  delay-matched p = 0 k = L variance at n = 60, L = 8, the pattern is "unital control not as modelled": UNRESOLVED, reported as a
  control finding. The test is two-sample (seeds 22991001 and 23191001 differ) and one-sided at 95 percent. Also state in part (2) that
  R2 is expected to be empty on the booked statistics.

In either case, say in part (1) that Question 2 is open on hardware, not in theory.

**M3. The H6 control when the dephasing variance is not resolved: settle it by rule and align the classifier.**

*Found.* The draft leaves this to Deviation 60 "or recorded as open" (Section 7, observation 1; Section 8). A rule chosen after data
would be post hoc. Deviation 60 now has part (6) (2e0fa64, draft 4a5b062; not reviewed here). When the dephasing interval's lower end
is ≤ 0 it decides the clause on bounds: `rule` 'bounds', `measured` None, `exceeds` = r_lo > max(d_hi, 0), and
`within` = r_lo ≤ F e^(1.96 s) d_hi.

`_control_reading` returns 'unresolved' whenever `measured` is not finite. It therefore reads every bounds-path control as
'unresolved', whatever `exceeds` and `within` say. R1 is unaffected, since it reads H6's result, but R2 and the UNRESOLVED reasons are
affected, and the reason text ("which H6 as coded counts as not exceeding") becomes false.

The row also lists "a dephasing variance not resolved above zero" among the UNRESOLVED patterns. That is false under part (6), where
R1 is reachable on bounds. It was already inaccurate at e9c25e6, because a positive point estimate with a lower end ≤ 0 gives a finite
ratio.

*Fix.*

1. Adopt part (6), or another rule, before review 05 reads H6, and delete "or recorded as open".
2. In `_control_reading`, read `rule` 'bounds' as 'as_modelled' when `exceeds` and `within` hold, 'partial' when `exceeds` holds and
   `within` does not, and 'unresolved' otherwise. R2 still needs a resolved dephasing variance. Correct the reason text.
3. Reword the row: an unresolved dephasing variance blocks R2 only.
4. Add a classifier test with a bounds-path control.
5. Rebase PR #8 on the `dev60-h7-analysis` head that carries part (6).
