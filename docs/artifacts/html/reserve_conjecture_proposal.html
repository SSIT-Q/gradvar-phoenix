Two things stated plainly

Hardware cannot prove or refute a conjecture, and this arm does not try. Cerezo et al.'s open ground is barren-plateau-free circuit families for which no classical simulator is known. A hardware-efficient ansatz at small angles is not in it: its simulability is proven (Angrisani et al. 2024) and warm-start training around it is known (Puig et al. 2024). So a TRACKS result validates a theorem at 85 qubits, and a LOST result is a statement about this device, this surrogate and this circuit. The new object is the transition map: where, as angles widen, per-draw classical tracking of hardware gradients fails.

The reserve is not free. The pre-registration's surplus rule sends freed minutes first to Deviation 17's M increases, then to the cut-order items, then to the reserve, and "never to new arms without a Deviation". Any option here needs a signed Deviation or its own pre-registration, and the Deviation 19 replications (L = 4 at n = 19; the n = 85, L = 8 flag if the run-day re-draw confirms it) come first.

Why the brief's full-angle ladder to L = 16–24 cannot be run as written. With Paper 1's uniform-angle draws and k = 1, the noiseless variance falls about 30× per four layers (1e-2 at L = 4, 2.5e-4 at L = 8, ~8e-6 predicted at L = 12). Extrapolated, L = 16 is ~3e-7 and L = 24 ~3e-10, below the 2e-6 null floor at any shot count; the shots needed to put the per-draw shot SE at half the gradient rms (S ≈ 2/Var) are 2.6e5 at L = 12 (57 min for M = 400) and 6.7e6 at L = 16 (~1,700 min). A depth-24 full-angle HEA with a local cost is the barren plateau, and a surrogate that predicts zero there is trivially right. The conjecture is about landscapes that provably have no plateau, so Option 1 runs its depth ladder in that regime (small-angle draws, a proven-simulable regime, so a check rather than a test) and puts the science in an angle ladder across the transition to full angles, where a surrogate can fail while the gradient is still measurable.

02Option 1 first choice Where does per-draw classical tracking of hardware gradients fail as the angles widen, at 85 qubits and up to 24 layers?

Critical prerequisite, no minutes involved. The repository has no per-draw surrogate at n = 85, L ≥ 8: gradvar/pauliprop.py is a second-moment (variance) engine, and per-draw gradients exist only by statevector on light cones of ≤ 24 qubits or by small MPS. Blocks A, B, D, E and F cannot be pre-registered until a coefficient-level, actual-angle truncated Pauli propagation (weight and coefficient cutoffs, noise channels from the run-day calibration) is built and validated: (i) against exact statevector on the n = 19 patch at L = 8–24 and σA; (ii) ℓ → ℓ + 2 stability at n = 85, L = 24; (iii) truncation error versus σ at L = 12 out to σ = 1.2. Estimate: about one to two weeks of one person's classical work plus a few CPU-days, and the theorist's certificate at the end. Block C needs none of this (its surrogate is Paper 1's existing statevector rebuild on the L = 8 cone and a converged propagation at L = 10).

What is proven, what is open, what this measures

Cerezo et al. (Nat. Commun. 16, 7907, 2025; arXiv:2312.09121) argue that the structure which provably removes barren plateaus can be used to simulate the loss classically in polynomial time, possibly with a one-off quantum data-collection step. For a hardware-efficient ansatz with a local cost, the plateau-free regimes are shallow depth (L ≲ log n) and small-angle draws at any depth, and both are already proven simulable by truncated Pauli propagation (Angrisani et al., arXiv:2409.01706; Rudolph et al. 2025). Pauli-path methods have also reproduced 127-qubit hardware expectation values in small-angle regimes (the utility-experiment reanalyses: Kim et al. 2023 data; Begušić, Gray and Chan 2024; Tindall et al. 2024; Rudolph's LOWESA). What no one has published, as far as the 21 Sep search shows, is a per-draw map of hardware gradients against a surrogate across the angle transition at this scale: at which σ, and how sharply, tracking fails, and whether the gradient is still measurable there.

Pre-registered predictions

Same n = 85 patch, cost and shifted-pair convention as Paper 1; draws θ ~ N(0, σ²). Surrogates and what "stops tracking" means

  • S2, the reference the verdict is read against: the noiseless per-draw surrogate (the new coefficient-level truncated Pauli propagation, or exact statevector / MPS where the cone allows), on the committed seeds, computed before retrieval by a worker barred from the run data (Deviation 54 pattern); converged at a point if raising ℓ by 2 moves the per-draw gradients by < 3 % rms, else the point is INDETERMINATE. S1, reported beside it: the same engine with the run-day non-unital noise model; the S1-versus-hardware residual is the noise-model residual and says whether the calibrated model accounts for what S2 misses. It never enters the tracking verdict.
  • Per point, on the same M draws: slope β of hardware on S2 (free, absorbs the attenuation; expected 0.85–1.0 at L ≤ 4 today) with 95 % paired-bootstrap CI; residual variance R = Var(ghw − β gS2); per-draw shot variance Vshot summed from the two shifted-circuit binomials, (1 − ⟨ZZ⟩+²)/(4S) + (1 − ⟨ZZ⟩−²)/(4S); τ = (R − Vshot) / Var(ghw), the fraction of the hardware gradient the noiseless surrogate does not explain. Today's data give τ ≈ 0.03 at L = 4 (n = 19). Power: with residuals near the shot level, SE(τ) ≈ √(2/M) × Vshot/Varhw ≈ 0.008 at M = 500 and a 30× floor margin, so the 0.10 / 0.25 thresholds are separated by > 10 SE. Paper 1's mean-as-null test (mean gradient consistent with zero per point) is carried over as a sanity check on the draw convention.
TRACKSτ ≤ 0.10 with upper CI ≤ 0.20, β within [0.6, 1.1], point claimable (Varhw − Varnull > 3σ), S2 converged. LOSTτ ≥ 0.25 with lower CI > 0.10, point claimable, S2 converged, no drift between run halves; σ* (or L*) is the first such point. The noise-model residual is reported next to it and decides only how the point is described (accounted for by the calibrated model, or not), never whether it is LOST. INDETERMINATEanything else: unconverged surrogate, point at the floor, drift between halves of the run (slope on first and last 100 draws differing by > 0.1).

03Option 2 second choice Is the attenuation-versus-depth curve the exponential that noise-induced barren plateaus predict, with the slope set by independently measured gate errors?

Theory under test

Wang et al. (Nat. Commun. 12, 6961, 2021; arXiv:2007.14384) prove that under local Pauli noise the gradient is bounded by a factor decaying exponentially in circuit depth, so it vanishes exponentially in n when depth grows linearly in n. The bound is an upper bound; the quantitative content testable here is the shape and rate of the attenuation A(L) = Varhw/Varnoiseless on the same draws. Schmitt et al. (arXiv:2602.22851, Feb 2026) report on IBM hardware at 8–102 qubits that gradient norms plateau rather than decay with accumulated noise, attributing it to T1-dominated noise; a per-draw attenuation curve against a calibrated model adjudicates between the two pictures on this device.

Falsifiable predictions

  1. P1 log A(L) is linear in L over L = 1 to 8 (exponential preferred over a power law or saturation by ΔAIC > 4).
  2. P2 the fitted rate c agrees within ±25 % with the Pauli-propagation noise model built from the same-day calibration (CZ error, T1/T2, readout) and with the identity-ladder decay measured on the day.
  3. P3 the ratio c(n = 37)/c(n = 19) equals the model's light-cone ratio within the fit CI (Wang's n-dependence enters through the cone).
