IBM Nighthawk r2 (ibm_phoenix): what changes the plan, and what saves minutes

A read of the calibration snapshots, the raw backend properties, the two pre-registrations and IBM's public documentation, aimed at one question: which settings on this processor improve the pre-registered measurements or cut QPU minutes without moving an estimand. Every device fact carries a source; every candidate carries a Deviation verdict.

Snapshots: CSV 11:13 UTC (Slack upload F0C3WJ98DPA) · raw properties 14:08 UTC (repo Action)Ledger: 360 Flex minutes; instance cap 220 min for ibm_phoenixPre-registrations Q1 v0.6 and P2 v0.2 read, not edited

The headline is not an optimisation, it is arithmetic. Paper 1's pre-registered grid is about 2.9×108 executions (Section 2: "shot total of order 2e8"). At the 250 µs rep_delay the Section 3b and Paper 2 budgets assume, IBM's own formula gives roughly 1,210 minutes for Paper 1 (main grid alone 584 min) against the 200 booked. At a 1 µs rep_delay it is about 27 minutes; at the 9.1 µs per circuit implied by the dashboard's 109.7 kHz maximum circuit rate, about 43 minutes. The Q1 text silently assumes the fast rate for Paper 1 ("110k circuits per second, so the shot budget is not expected to be binding") while the dial arm and Paper 2 book at 250 µs. The plan is feasible only if backend.rep_delay_range on ibm_phoenix reaches the microsecond floor. Reading that field costs zero QPU minutes and should happen before anything else.

Raw properties (gz) versus the CSV: what the CSV cannot tell you

  • Per-edge ZZ and J couplings. general carries zz_ and jq_ for all 218 edges: median |ZZ| 26.7 kHz, J median 0.46 MHz. The dial's 400 ns idle therefore accrues a mask-dependent phase of about 0.07 rad per idle neighbour pair; Section 3b names this contaminant without a number. Use these values in the noisy simulation instead of a guess.
  • Qubit 17 is not just dead, it is collided. ZZ fits of −4.96 MHz to qubit 18 and +4.29 MHz to qubit 27 (200× the median). Qubit 27 sits in row 2, inside the 10×10 ladder patch (n = 90), with T1 56 µs, T2 42 µs, 1Q error 1.1e-3 (5× median) and CZ errors 7.5–8.1e-3 on all three edges. It passes every pre-registered cut. Qubit 18 (T2 29 µs) is outside the patches.
  • Initialisation error outliers. Median 2.0e-5, but qubits 8, 22, 49, 77 sit at 1e-3 to 8e-3, and 17, 24, 47, 62 have no value. Qubit 22 (row 2) is inside the n = 90 patch and has no pre-registered cut against it. For an experiment whose estimand is a variance of expectation values, a 400×-median state-preparation error on one patch qubit is a small bias but a logged one.
  • IBM's own layer-fidelity chains. general_qlists lists lf_4 … lf_100, the chains IBM used for the EPLG figure; lf_100 is a ready-made 100-qubit path through the clean region (bottom rows, avoiding 55/62/63/72/73). The team's 45-second heuristic chain search can be checked against it.
  • Snapshot drift. T1 in the 14:08 properties differs from the 11:13 CSV by a median 14 µs and a maximum 107 µs on one qubit. The repo's 15:54 and 15:59 CSVs have blank Init error and MEASURE/MEASURE_2 columns, so the CSV path loses information the gz keeps.

5. r2-specific items that change the plan

  • Qubit 17: dead (no T1/T2, readout 0.506, no calibrated CZ, 4 blank init) and collided with 18 and 27 (ZZ fits of ±5 MHz). Exclude 27 from the n = 90 patch by Deviation; 18 is already outside every patch.
  • High-error edges: 21 edges above 1e-2; cluster 55/61/62/63/72/73 already excluded; (100,101) 0.044, (95,96) 0.037, (98,99) 0.018, (87,97) 0.019, (86,87) 0.013 all sit inside the bottom-anchored ladder patches and are not excluded by the rule. Their contribution is in the pre-drawn curves if the simulation uses the run-day edge list, which it must.
  • Qubit 79 reset 2140 ns: row 7, column 9; inside the 6×10, 8×10 and 10×10 patches. The dial excludes it from dial patches (Section 3b); the Paper 1 grid does not need to. If the dial's n = 60 patch is 6×10 rows 6–11, qubit 79 is in it and the patch becomes 55 qubits, not 56.
  • Initialisation outliers 8, 22, 49, 77: 22 is inside the n = 90 patch (Deviation to add init_error > 1e-3 to the run-day cut).
  • xslow (1000 ns) and measure_2 (1140 ns) exist in the target; neither is documented; neither is used by the plan.
  • Fractional rx exposed: hardware.py loads with use_fractional_gates=False, which is right; keep it.
  • rep_delay floor unknown: r1's fake config says 20 µs; IBM's r2 marketing says 1 µs; nothing fetched gives ibm_phoenix's range. The whole ledger turns on this number.
  • Estimator and reset: docs say Estimator does not support dynamic circuits; a bare reset may or may not be classed as one. Kill 3b(d) is live and testable on Marrakesh tonight.
  • 15 October maintenance: timezone unconfirmed; keep the day and its neighbours free.
  • 3-hour per-job cap: any single job above 180 min QPU usage fails and is charged; split jobs at ≤ 60 min.

6. Do not do

  • Do not book the Paper 1 grid at a 250 µs rep_delay; it costs six times the allocation.
  • Do not co-run patches to save minutes; at microsecond rep_delay it saves 2 minutes and costs the ladder's noise homogeneity.
  • Do not enable fractional gates, dynamical decoupling, or xslow anywhere in Paper 1 or the dial.
  • Do not use session mode; do not put more than ~60 QPU-minutes in one job.
  • Do not cut shots or M; if minutes are freed, spend them on Deviation 17's M increase and on the Section 3b cut-order items in their fixed order.
  • Do not use init_qubits=False outside Paper 2's one pre-registered Q1 job.
  • Do not switch to measure_2 in October, even if selectable; it changes the readout channel under test.
  • Do not plan patches from the 11:13 CSV alone; re-derive from the run-day properties (T1 drift up to 107 µs in three hours) and keep the gz.
  • Do not read the 25× initialisation claim as a reset-error figure; the target has no reset gate_error, and Paper 2 exists to measure it.
