1The answer in seven lines

  1. Recommendation. 2Where the field stands in September 2026

Theory. For deep hardware-efficient circuits the gradient variance is set by the dimension of the circuit's Lie algebra and is exponentially small in the qubit count (2309.09342, 2309.07902; review 2405.00781). For shallow circuits with local observables the decay is only polynomial up to depth about log n (2001.00550). The shallow, local regime is therefore the one where a signal can sit above a shot-noise floor.

Noise. Under Pauli-type noise, gradients decay exponentially with depth (2007.14384). Its Theorem 1 bounds the derivative itself, for all parameters, by sqrt(8 ln 2) N_O ||omega||_inf ||eta||_1 sqrt(n) q^(cL+1) with c = 1/(2 ln 2) = 0.7213. At n = 100 and q = 0.998 that is about 23 for every L up to 12, so it is uninformative on our ladder because of the sqrt(n) prefactor and the weak per-layer noise, not because of any 2^(n/2) factor (an earlier version of this memo said so and was wrong). The tighter unital bound is Mele et al. Proposition 10: Var[dC] at most exp(-Omega(|P| + L)) under any unital noise, with no n prefactor. Quantitative predictions for ibm_phoenix still come from our own noisy simulation with the device calibration. Under non-unital noise (T1 amplitude damping, which Heron and Nighthawk both have), Mele et al. (2403.13927, Nature Physics 2026) show that, on average over 2-design gate ensembles, local non-unital noise acting after every layer leaves local-observable costs with order-1 variance and only the parameters in the last order-log-n layers with non-vanishing gradient variance. Singkanipa and Lidar (2402.08721, Quantum 2025) reach the same conclusion for amplitude damping. An earlier draft of this memo said these two papers disagree; on reading them they agree, so there is no theory dispute for hardware to adjudicate. The only 50+ qubit hardware gradient study, Schmitt et al. (2602.22851, preprint), measured a gradient-norm proxy at 8 to 102 qubits, mostly on Eagle devices, and saw saturation rather than decay.

Simulability. Cerezo et al. (2312.09121, a perspective) present case-by-case evidence that structures which remove plateaus also tend to permit classical simulation; they do not claim a theorem. Pauli propagation now simulates typical circuits under local noise, with a 121-qubit demonstration that is heuristic and outside the theorem's regime (2409.01706, 2501.13101, 2411.19896). A referee will ask for a classical baseline beside any hardware claim.

Scale. Every 100+ qubit variational result in the literature is gradient-free (2508.13557, 2312.00997, 2405.09169, 2405.05068, 2412.07915). Gradient-based training at that scale is currently unaffordable, which is part of why the measurement is worth making.

3The disorder idea, specifically

Published in outline on hardware by 2404.17560 (31 qubits, 1D ring and circulant graphs, 4096 shots per gradient) and as noiseless numerics by Li and Yin (Frontiers of Physics 20, 023202, 2025: variance constant in n to 18 qubits and in depth to 250 layers for a narrow-kick Floquet circuit with full-range random Z and ZZ angles; no exponent fit, no error bars; their Fig. 2b shows that full-width quenched randomness with a thermal kick still gives exponential decay with depth). Theory by Park, Kang and Huh (2403.04844): the small-angle condition is a theorem, and its Theorem 1 already covers heavy-hex; the localisation condition is argued, not proven. Numerics by 2407.11589 (6 to 16 qubits) and 2506.13865 (5 to 10 qubits, analog quench ansatz, two disorder values: localisation delays the onset of the plateau in depth, but the variance saturates to the same small value in both phases).

Open: whether quenched site-to-site randomness matters at fixed angle width, the fitted exponent as a function of window width, any 2D graph (heavy-hex or square lattice), anything above 31 qubits, and a matched MPS baseline.

4The device: ibm_phoenix (Nighthawk r2)

ibm_phoenix is IBM's Nighthawk r2 processor: 120 programmable qubits on a 12 by 10 square lattice joined by 218 tunable couplers, so a qubit has up to four neighbours rather than the two or three of heavy-hex. The 458 physical elements are the 120 qubits, the 218 couplers and a dissipative reset element on every qubit. Basis gates are cz, id, rx, rz, sx, x and xslow. Mid-circuit reset and dynamic circuits are supported. With fractional rx gates enabled, ZNE via probabilistic error amplification and gate twirling are unsupported, so keep fractional gates off for the mitigation sweep.

Reading the map

Every mitigation row has an empty hardware column above 50 qubits. The only 50+ qubit measurements of anything gradient-like are 2602.22851 (a gradient-norm proxy, not Var[dC], mostly on Eagle) and 2604.19426 (a landscape-span metric at p=1). Nothing at all has been published on Nighthawk r2, and no gradient measurement exists on any Nighthawk. The non-unital row is not a dispute: 2403.13927 and 2402.08721 agree that under local non-unital noise after every layer local-observable costs keep order-1 variance on average, with only the last order-log-n layers carrying non-vanishing gradients. At Nighthawk noise (T1 about 178 us, about 0.3 us per layer) the proven floor is about 1e-11, so the floor itself is not hardware-testable; the layer-index dependence is. The usable two-qubit depth on the full ibm_torino lattice is about 18, not 28; no such benchmark exists for Nighthawk r2, so the Question 1 depth ceiling is measured in the dry run. The disorder row, which is the team's original idea, is not empty: 2404.17560 did the kicked, disorder-narrowed HEA on IBM hardware to 31 qubits and 2407.11589 and 2506.13865 confirm it numerically, so the idea itself is published. What remains open there is the confound between small angles and quenched randomness, the fitted exponent b(W), the 2D case, 40 to 120 qubits, and a matched classical baseline.

Question 1. Noise-induced gradient concentration on Nighthawk r2, measured by parameter shift

For a Ry/CZ ansatz on the 12 by 10 square lattice with a two-local ZZ observable on ibm_phoenix: how does parameter-shift Var[dC] scale with depth (1 to about 12 two-qubit layers; the lower CZ error may allow deeper, so set the ceiling from the dry run, since no depth benchmark exists for Nighthawk r2), qubit count (20, 40, 60, 80, 100) and error-mitigation level (resilience 0, 1, 2) at matched total shots? The measured curve is compared with three predictions: the noiseless light-cone value from simulation, which is n-independent at fixed depth once the patch contains the observable's causal cone, so L = 1 and 2 are n-independent by construction and L of 8 or more covers every patch on the ladder (2011.10530); the unital-noise simulation calibrated to ibm_phoenix, which decays exponentially in depth (Mele et al. Proposition 10); and the non-unital layer-index signature, in which the variance depends on the layer index k at fixed L and the last order-log-n layers decay more slowly (2403.13927, 2402.08721). 2602.22851 saw saturation of a gradient-norm proxy and is the nearest hardware comparison. Nearest existing work: 2602.22851 measures a gradient-norm proxy to 102 qubits, mostly on Eagle, with no mitigation sweep and no confidence intervals; 2604.19426 measures a landscape-span metric on ibm_fez at p=1 with ZNE but no depth scan and no gradients; 2007.14384's own hardware data stops at 9 qubits.

Budget: 5 qubit counts by 4 depths by 3 resilience levels, 60 points (the torino grid had 72 with a 127-qubit rung); M = 200 parameter draws by 2 shift circuits by 4096 shots per point, about 1e8 shots in total, with ZNE at level 2 on a reduced grid. ZNE inflates the shot variance of the gradient estimator by the squared 1-norm of its coefficients, 4x for linear and about 12x for Richardson extrapolation (Banchi et al.), while level-1 readout mitigation rescales landscape and shot variance by the same factor and leaves the signal-to-shot ratio unchanged (Aghaei Saem et al.), so the mitigation arm is stated as a ratio. The locked-time cost should be well under the earlier torino estimate given the 25x higher circuit throughput; measure locked time per point in the dry run before booking, and keep a reserve for calibration snapshots, reruns and the null-model control. No simulability defence is needed: the paper is a hardware characterisation, the regime is heuristically expected to be Pauli-propagation simulable, the average-case guarantees (2403.13927 Proposition 3, 2501.13101) do not cover our ensemble or precision, so the baseline is demonstrated numerically and reported as a feature, which is what 2312.09121 asks for. Breakthrough potential: moderate. It will not be a quantum-advantage result and should not be framed as one.

Question 2. Disorder or small angles: separating the two on heavy-hex

When the single-qubit angles of the same ansatz are drawn from a narrow window (the prescription of 2403.04844 and 2404.17560), is the preserved gradient variance due to quenched site-to-site randomness or simply to near-identity angles? 2404.17560 used a 1D ring and circulant graphs, so a 2D square lattice is new geometry for the disorder question, though 2403.04844's Theorem 1 already covers it; the contribution remains controlled numerics and hardware evidence. Following 2506.13865, fit both the decay exponent b(W) in Var[dC] ~ b(W)^-n and the depth at which the plateau sets in, as functions of window width W, on the 12 by 10 square lattice at 20 to 100 qubits. Hardware reuses the Question 1 ladder with three angle distributions (uniform, narrow, narrow plus fixed offsets) at levels 0 and 1, about 36 points, 90 to 100 minutes, with the memory-effect control suggested by 2409.16180. Report an MPS simulation at the same W, depth and n beside every hardware point and frame the result as locating where the classical surrogate stops converging; do not claim advantage. If quenched randomness turns out not to matter, the result reclassifies a Quantum-published mitigation as a small-angle initialisation, which is also publishable. Li and Yin (Frontiers of Physics 20, 023202, 2025; authors Xin Li and Zhang-qi Yin) add peer-reviewed noiseless numerics at 4 to 18 qubits and depth to 250 claiming variance constant in n and depth for a narrow-kick MBL Floquet ansatz, with no exponent fit and no error bars. Their Fig. 2b shows full-width angles with a thermal kick still decaying exponentially with depth, and their Fig. 6 shows MBL initialisation reaching worse minima. This narrows Question 2 toward the small-angle explanation and lowers its odds from moderate to high to moderate.

Question 3. Ancilla-reset dissipative ansatz versus matched unitary ansatz

Does an ansatz with periodic mid-circuit measurement-and-reset of ancillas (2507.02043) retain a larger parameter-shift gradient variance than a gate-count-matched unitary ansatz at 40 to 80 qubits, once reset error and added runtime are counted? This is the one escape route on the map with neither a simulability theorem against it nor any hardware data, and the same non-unital physics is what 2403.13927 says prevents a plateau for local costs, though because the circuit becomes effectively shallow; the same paper lists mid-circuit measurement and feedforward as outside its theorems. ibm_phoenix has a dissipative reset element on every qubit and supports mid-circuit reset, so the earlier kill criterion (reset not exposed on the device) no longer applies. The remaining kill criteria are reset error and latency, which are not in the calibration CSV and must be measured in the dry run before they enter the noise model; the simulation cost of non-unital ansaetze (density-matrix or trajectory to about 16 to 20 qubits, MPO beyond); and the mid-October deadline. Breakthrough potential: high if positive. It competes with Question 2 for the same gated minutes.

Question 4. Initialisation families head to head at fixed shot budget

Do identity-block (1903.05076), Gaussian (2203.09376) and a first-moment-distinct shifted family (2606.18515) initialisations of the same circuit give measurably different initial gradient variances and first-step descent directions at 40 to 100 qubits under a fixed shot budget? 2606.18515 proves exponentially many inequivalent plateau-free initialisation families analytically and has no hardware runs; its first-moment diagnostic screens families out rather than ranking them, so the simulation stage picks which three to run. Three initialisations by 3 qubit counts by 2 depths at level 1, 18 points, about 40 to 50 minutes, sharing circuits and calibration snapshots with Question 1. Near-identity circuits are within Pauli-propagation reach; state that openly and give the baseline.

Question 5. A new mitigation argued not to be classically simulable

Is there an ansatz or parameter regime that keeps gradients non-vanishing at 100+ qubits while demonstrably escaping the average-case Pauli-propagation theorems (2409.01706, 2501.13101) and the patch-surrogate construction (2411.19896)? The simulation stage would have to show a non-converging Pauli-path delta-sweep (2507.10771), a failing MPS baseline and a variance above the shot floor at 100 qubits, all under a realistic Heron noise model, which 2501.13101 gives strong reason to think will restore simulability. No team has met that burden. Not recommended as a paper; reserve the argument for the discussion section of Question 1.

Recommendation. One campaign with shared circuits: Question 1 is the main arm, about 60 percent of minutes. Questions 2 and 3 are gated arms, about 20 percent to whichever clears simulation first (both if minutes allow); 20 percent reserve. Question 4 shares Question 1's circuits and calibration snapshots.

7What the simulation stage must show before any QPU minute

  • A noiseless Var[dC] versus n curve at 4 to 24 qubits with 10^4 parameter draws, reproducing the standard of 2602.03291.
  • Noisy predictions from the ibm_phoenix calibration snapshot for every planned (n, depth) point, with the 4096- and 16384-shot floors drawn on the same plot, so the competing predictions are separated by more than the floor at the points booked.
  • A Pauli-propagation delta sweep (2507.10771) on the same circuits, which becomes the classical baseline in the paper.
  • For Question 2, evidence that varying only the quenched offsets at fixed angle width changes the fitted exponent by more than the fit uncertainty.

Two gates follow from this. Gate 1: no hardware minute is booked until the simulation separates the predictions. Gate 2: hardware points that sit within the measured noise floor (the null model of 2602.22851, cost replaced by random spectrum draws) are reported as floor, not as signal.
