Does a reset ansatz beat barren plateaus on ibm_phoenix?

Literature check, four candidate ansätze, exact simulation to 12 qubits, hardware costing, and odds. Frank by request.

Verdict: the breakthrough as framed does not survive. All three legs of the starting claim fail against the literature, and the simulation shows why: resets cut the light cone, which anti-concentrates the cost while making every parameter more than a few layers from the output untrainable, and the surviving circuit is exactly what Pauli propagation simulates best. What does survive is a well-posed hardware test nobody has run (Section 6). Odds that the original idea yields a PRX Quantum/Quantum paper: about 5%. Odds for the reframed test as a stand-alone paper: 10 to 15%; as an arm of Paper 1: 70%.

1. The starting claim against the literature

Leg 1: "Mele et al. exclude measurement-and-reset." Only half true. Mele et al. (2403.13927, Nat. Phys. 2026) model noise as one single-qubit channel N on every qubit after every 2-design layer, parametrised by c = (‖D‖² + ‖t‖²)/3. Reset to |0⟩ is amplitude damping with γ = 1: t = (0,0,1), D = 0, c = 1/3. Theorem 1 (effective log depth) and Proposition 3 (the classical estimator) need only c < 1, so they cover reset. Theorem 5 (Θ(1) variance of local costs) assumes only non-unital noise with ‖t‖₂ = Θ(1) and so formally covers reset too. Theorem 8 and Corollary 9 (Var[∂C] = exp(−Θ(L−k)); Θ(1) gradient norm) are the ones that exclude a replacer channel, stated in the paper as noise that "does not output a fixed state". The reason for that exclusion is ours to state, not theirs: a channel with a fixed output erases every derivative upstream of it, so a full-register reset makes the bound vacuous. What their theorems do not cover is reset on a subset of qubits per layer. That is the only genuine gap, and it is the case simulated below. Singkanipa and Lidar (2402.08721, Quantum 2025) define HS-contractive non-unital maps by ‖N(ρ₁)−N(ρ₂)‖₂ ≤ r‖ρ₁−ρ₂‖₂; reset is the r = 0 member, inside their framework, and they extend the parameter-shift rule to noisy channels, which we can cite for gradient validity.

Leg 2: "No trainability result for dissipative or measurement-based ansätze." False. The table lists what exists. The decisive item is Shirgure, Kökcü and Niu (2606.23751, June 2026): with purification and Pauli-path analysis they show that feedforward, reset and engineered-dissipation gadgets anti-concentrate the cost through Pauli terms supported on ancillas whose coefficients depend only on gadget parameters, while upstream parameters stay exponentially untrainable; their conclusion, argued through Stinespring purification rather than proved as a theorem, is that building a barren-plateau-free dynamic circuit is at least as hard as building a barren-plateau-free unitary ansatz from first principles.

Leg 3: "No simulability result covers mid-circuit reset." False, and reset is the easiest case. In the Heisenberg picture the reset adjoint maps Z → I, X → 0, Y → 0: Pauli weight can only fall. Angrisani, Mele et al. (2501.13101, PRX Quantum 2026) prove Pauli propagation efficient for any local noise including amplitude damping with γ ∈ (0, 1], i.e. including reset, and state that their results also cover dynamic non-unitary operations of the Deshpande type when applied at a constant rate; Martinez et al. (2501.13050, PRL 2025) prove the same for Pauli backpropagation under non-unital noise; Deshpande et al. themselves note that, with high probability over the initial parameters, their dynamic circuits are efficiently simulable by low-weight Pauli-path propagation. The correct statement of what is open is narrower than the programme's claim: reset circuits at random initialisation are simulable on average; structured reset circuits can be hard (Angrisani et al. point to the quantum-refrigerator scheme as an engineered non-unital circuit that is not simulable, and Fefferman et al., 2306.16659, PRX Quantum 2024, show non-unital noise removes anti-concentration at every depth, with consequences for both hardness and easiness); and a circuit that is trainable at random initialisation is, by construction, one that sits near the average case. That is the Cerezo et al. dichotomy, not an escape from it.

2. Candidate ansätze on phoenix (CZ + per-qubit reset, 2×W patches)

All use RY·RZ on every qubit (2 parameters per qubit per layer), then CZ on every patch edge (three parallel sublayers: even and odd horizontal pairs, vertical pairs). Cost is ⟨Zd0Zd1⟩ on an adjacent data pair in row 0. Parameter shift with ±π/2 stays exact for channels: the cost is affine in the superoperator of each gate, so it is a + b cos θ + c sin θ in any single angle whatever the resets do (Singkanipa–Lidar give the noisy-channel version).

(i) Dissipative HEA, checkerboard

At the start of layers 2..L reset the qubits with (row + col + ℓ) even: every qubit is reset every second layer.

(iv) Feedforward (Deshpande-type: RX on the data neighbour conditioned on the ancilla outcome) is a superset of (iii); every simulability argument above applies to it and 2606.23751 treats it directly, so it was not simulated.

What the scalings would mean. Θ(1) early-layer variance in n and L would be a genuine escape and, per Cerezo et al. (2312.09121), would demand a simulability check. Polynomial decay would match Zapusek's bound. Exponential decay in the distance to the output at a constant rate α (Mele's Theorem 8 form) is what the light-cone argument predicts: a Pauli path reaching an early parameter must never carry X or Y on a qubit at the moment it is reset, each reset layer removes a constant fraction of paths, and the cost depends only on the last Θ(1/α) layers, which Pauli propagation handles with a smaller path budget than the noiseless circuit.

3. Simulation

Exact, noiseless, ensemble-statevector simulation with explicit trace-and-replace resets (cross-checked against qiskit-aer density matrices to 1e-10), n = 4..12 on 2×W patches, L = 1..8, M = 200 uniform parameter draws, ±π/2 parameter-shift gradients of ⟨Zd0Zd1⟩ with respect to the RY on d0 in the first layer (k = 1), the middle layer (k = ⌈L/2⌉) and the last layer (k = L), bootstrap 95% CIs (2000 resamples). Total compute 44 CPU-minutes on 4 shared cores. Files: paper2/paper2_gradvar_results.csv, paper2/sim2.py.

Headlines. At n = 12, L = 8 the first-layer variance is 1.21e-03 for the plain HEA, 8.3e-04 for the random-half dissipative HEA, 2.9e-04 for the ancilla-row reset and 2.7e-11 for the checkerboard. No reset variant is above the plain HEA at any n or L in the grid; the ancilla-row reset is below it by 5.7x at n = 4, 5.2x at n = 6, 4.0x at n = 8, 7.2x at n = 10, 4.2x at n = 12 (first-layer parameter), and by similar factors for the last-layer parameter and for Var[C]. The k-profile is flat. At L = 8 the ratio Var[∂C] last layer / first layer is 1.6 / 2.3 / 1.5 / 4.3 / 3.6 for the ancilla-row reset (n = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12), 2.4 / 2.3 / 2.6 / 2.2 / 2.3 for the HEA and 1.6 / 4.7 / 1.4 / 4.5 / 2.0 for the random half; the mid-layer point sits between (table). A Mele Theorem 8 profile would put the last layer at Θ(1) with the first layer exponentially below it, a ratio growing like e0.8·7 ≈ 300 at these rates; the measured ratios are 1 to 5. What decays is everything, with L. At n = 8 the ancilla-row reset's Var[C] falls at 0.66 per layer, its last-layer variance at 0.70 and its first-layer variance at 0.74 (fit L = 3..8; the HEA's Var[C] falls at 0.38), all toward the Haar value 1/(2n+1) ≈ 3.9e-3 for n = 8. That is ordinary depth-driven concentration of a small 2×W patch, the same for every ansatz, not a non-unital layer-index profile. The checkerboard is the exception only because its observed pair is reset within two layers of the output (below). The resets did not fight the plateau here; on this patch they added nothing that a shallower unitary circuit would not.

Why (i) looks two layers deep. Backward from the output, Zd0 passes through the CZ layer unchanged and the rotation turns it into cos θ Z + sin θ X on d0. By construction d0 is reset before layer L and d1 before layer L−1, so Z → I and X, Y → 0 on the observed pair within two layers, and the cost is, to leading order, a function of the last two layers' angles. The light cone is not cut (the cone reaches layer 1 at every L in the table) and no gradient is identically zero (frac_exact_zero ≤ 1 in 200 draws for every parameter): the surviving Pauli paths, those where an X first spread a Z onto a neighbour through CZ and the next rotation turned the origin's X into a Z before its reset, are suppressed at e−3.3 per layer (fit L = 3..8 at n = 8: e−3.3; L = 4..8 at n = 12: e−2.7), so by L = 8 the first-layer variance is 1e-10 to 1e-11, dead for any shot budget. The large Var[C] (Var[C] = 0.15 at n = 12) is trivial by observable placement: a depth-2 function of a few angles has O(1) variance, and comparing it with a depth-8 HEA says nothing about the plateau.

What was cut and why. The grid is n = 4..12 at L = 8, L = 1..8 at n = 8, and L = 4 at n = 12; the n = 12 depth scan (L = 1..7) was dropped on the coordinator's time check, so αL at n = 12 is a two-point estimate and the fitted rates are quoted at n = 8 with their fit range. The mid-layer parameter was added in the rerun after review. The feedforward variant (iv) was not simulated. Everything is noiseless: reset error, readout error and T1 during a 2 µs measure-based reset are not in the model.

4. The simulability trade-off

The data-row entropy (bottom right of the figure) says the ancilla-reset ansatz delivers a data row that is nearly maximally mixed (5.83 of 6 bits at n = 12), i.e. the resets act on the data row as strong non-unital noise, the regime of Mele's Theorem 1. The checkerboard ansatz keeps the state purer (2.64 bits) because it never lets entanglement build. Neither direction is favourable: mixed means noise-simulable, pure-and-shallow means light-cone-simulable. The honest proxy is the Pauli-path budget, and with the reset rule Z → I, X/Y → 0 that budget is strictly smaller than for the same circuit without resets. The Cerezo et al. dichotomy is not evaded here; it is illustrated.

One loophole, weak but real. Every simulability theorem above assumes independent layers. A parameter-shared Floquet dissipative ansatz (Dowarah et al., 2605.30572: one parametrised layer repeated, so the deep circuit is a fixed channel iterated to its fixed point) makes every parameter a last-layer parameter, and the gradient becomes a derivative of the channel's fixed point, which Theorem 8 does not address. It is still a shallow-effective-circuit story (the fixed point is reached in O(1/α) iterations and is what Pauli propagation converges to), so I rate it as unlikely to yield hardness, but it is the one variant a referee could say we did not test.

5. Sharpest testable hypothesis

H2 (two parts). On ibm_phoenix, for the ancilla-row reset ansatz (iii) and a gate-count-matched plain HEA on 2×W patches, W = 6 and 10, L = 4 and 8, with parameter-shift variances at k = 1 and k = L over 200 draws: (a) depth decay. Var[C], Var[∂k=1C] and Var[∂k=LC] all fall between L = 4 and L = 8 at a common rate for both ansätze (simulation, n = 8, fit L = 3..8: αL = 0.74 for the reset ansatz, 0.47 for the HEA); the reset ansatz is never above the HEA. (b) layer-index profile. At L = 8 the ratio Var[∂k=LC] / Var[∂k=1C] is below 10 for both ansätze (simulation at n = 12: 3.6 reset, 2.3 HEA), i.e. there is no Θ(1) last layer over an exponentially suppressed first layer. A Pauli propagation with the reset rule Z → I, X/Y → 0 truncated at weight 6 reproduces all 8 hardware points within their bootstrap CIs after readout mitigation.

Falsifier (any one, at two or more points, outside the bootstrap CI): the reset ansatz's Var[∂k=1C] above the matched HEA's; a k = L / k = 1 ratio above 10 for the reset ansatz at L = 8; Var[C] of the reset ansatz flat in L while the HEA's falls; or a Pauli-propagation residual outside the CI. Any of these would be the interesting outcome and would reopen the question. [Struck 19 Sep 2026 after theorist review: a truncation residual does not imply hardness; average-case simulability is proven (Angrisani et al. 2501.13101).] Prediction, frankly: none fires.

A first hardware measurement of a dynamic-circuit gradient landscape and a direct test of anti-concentration-without-trainability at 20 to 100 qubits: not a breakthrough, a clean falsification test with a classical baseline, which is what Question 3 of the decision memo should become.
