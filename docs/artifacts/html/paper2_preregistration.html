1Motivation and novelty

Nighthawk r2 adds a dissipative reset element to each of its 120 qubits. On ibm_phoenix this appears as a native reset instruction of 400 ns on 119 qubits (qubit 79: 2140 ns) alongside two measurement-based variants, measure_reset (1940 ns) and measure_reset_2 (1140 ns). The target reports no gate_error for any of the three; the nearest calibration figure is the initialisation error, median 2e-5, which bounds the reset error only on the assumption, not verified, that IBM measures it with the reset element engaged. Nothing has been published on Nighthawk r2 or ibm_phoenix (arXiv metadata search, 19 September 2026; all six Nighthawk papers found used r1). A per-qubit map of reset error, of dissipative against measurement-based reset, and of what a reset does to idle neighbours would be the first independent characterisation of this device class; IBM will publish its own.

The reset is also a resource. Paper 1's non-unital arm uses pooled random-mask resets as a dial on the non-unital strength of the noise; the dial is only meaningful if the reset realises the intended channel (Q4) and leaves the other qubits alone (Q2, Q3). Schmitt et al. (2602.22851) attribute the saturation of gradient magnitudes on IBM hardware to T1-dominated non-unital noise; there the strength was set by the device's T1, not by a knob, and a calibrated reset is what turns that observation into a controlled experiment. Dynamic circuits reuse auxiliary qubits by resetting them mid-circuit, and the disturbance a mid-circuit operation inflicts on spectators is an open characterisation problem (for one recent framing, Sramek, 2603.18381, tested on a synthetic harness).

The protocols come from the mid-circuit-measurement (MCM) literature. Govia et al. (2207.04836): a randomized-benchmarking suite that detects measurement-induced errors on spectator qubits and entangling errors between measured and spectator qubits, run on an IBM Falcon device. Hines and Proctor (2406.09299): MCM cycle benchmarking, learning Pauli error rates in randomly compiled layers of MCMs and Clifford gates, including correlated errors. Zhang et al. (2406.02669): a generalised cycle-benchmarking algorithm and a theory of which MCM noise parameters are learnable under SPAM noise. Chu et al. (2606.00433): three repeated MCMs on a maximally mixed input determine all learnable parameters of a Z-twirled single-qubit MCM instrument up to one gauge freedom, with T1-decay-dominated backaction found on IBM processors. None has been applied to a dissipative (non-measurement) reset or to a square-lattice device; adapting them is this paper's methodological content.

2Questions and hypotheses

Every statistic and threshold below is fixed before data. ⟨Z⟩ = +1 for |0⟩. "Per qubit" means each of the 119 operational qubits; qubit 17 (no T1 or T2, readout error 0.51, no calibrated coupler) is excluded everywhere.

Q1. Reset-error map: native reset against measure_reset and measure_reset_2 Protocol, 118 qubits in parallel per circuit (qubit 79, whose reset is 2140 ns, runs its native-reset arms (a), (b) and (f) in a separate 3-circuit job at the same shots so that no parallel circuit is padded to it; Deviation 6): (a) X → reset → measure and (b) reset → measure for each of the three reset kinds; (c) X → resetm → measure for m = 2 and 4 (native); (d) X → measure and measure alone, the day's readout confusion; (e) X → delay → measure, the no-reset reference, with delay 400 ns (2140 ns on qubit 79); (f) |+⟩ → reset → measure in X for each kind, the residual coherence. Sampler init_qubits is True (the device's own pre-shot initialisation) for these 14 circuits, so arm (b) measures the residual after initialisation plus one reset and is reported as such; a second job repeats arm (b) with init_qubits False (3 circuits), logged, as the only look at the pre-shot state. The reset error is εq = P(1 | X, reset, measure) − P(1 | measure), assuming a preceding reset leaves the readout error unchanged; raw probabilities are reported alongside, since Zhang et al. show some MCM noise parameters are inseparable from SPAM and Chu et al. find a one-parameter gauge. The m-dependence separates per-attempt failure (residual falls geometrically) from steady-state heating (residual constant), the analogue of Chu et al.'s repeated-measurement design. H1 Primary, a device-level test: the median native reset error over the 119 qubits is ≤ 3e-4 (predicted ≤ 1e-4 from the initialisation error; median floor 3.2e-5, so the prediction itself is resolvable at 3×). Secondary: εq ≤ 1e-3 on at least 108 of 119 qubits (per-qubit floor 2.8e-4). Ordering of medians native < measure_reset_2 < measure_reset, predicted from P(0|1) = 1.4e-2 and the MEASURE_2 error 6.4e-3. Refuted if the median's 95% interval lies above 3e-4, or fewer than 108 point estimates fall at or below 1e-3, or the ordering is violated outside the intervals.
Q2. Spectator backaction of a reset on idle neighbours Protocol: five target masks Ms = {q = 10i + j : (i + 2j) mod 5 = s}, s = 0…4. Each mask holds exactly 24 nominal targets, no two within lattice distance 3, so every non-target has at most one target neighbour and each qubit is a target in one mask; M0 has 22 acting targets, since dead qubit 17 and qubit 79 (removed from the parallel arms by Deviation 6) both fall in it, and the five masks cover the 422 directed neighbour pairs among the 118 remaining qubits once, the 6 directed pairs involving qubit 79 being unmeasured in Q2. Neighbours are prepared in |+⟩ (|0⟩ for the excitation channel), the target in |0⟩ or |1⟩; r ∈ {1, 4, 16} repetitions of reset or delay (400 ns; 2140 ns for target 79) act on the target while the neighbours idle; neighbours are read in X, Y or Z. Four arms per (mask, r, axis): {target |0⟩, |1⟩} × {reset, delay}. Primary statistic: the target-|0⟩ excess, reset arm minus delay arm, per reset from the slope in r; here the target is in |0⟩ throughout both arms, so the state-dependent ZZ phase cancels and any excess is backaction. Secondary: the target-|1⟩ excess after subtracting the ZZ mismatch: a working reset empties the target after an effective time τeff (about 25 ns if IBM's figure holds), so the spectator accrues ζ × (400 ns − τeff) less ZZ phase than in the delay arm, up to 12 mrad at ζ = 2π × 5 kHz and 118 mrad at 2π × 50 kHz. This mismatch is not backaction, and τeff is what this paper measures rather than assumes (Deviation 4): ζ is measured per pair from the two delay arms, φdelay(1) − φdelay(0) = ζ × 400 ns (ζ convention: ζ = 2π × the ZZ coupling in Hz, interaction exp(−i ζτ/4 ZZ), conditional phase ζτ, shared with Paper 1 Deviation 34), the |1⟩-arm excess phase is regressed on ζ across the directed pairs, the slope is τeff − 400 ns (a new deliverable, with its interval), and the residual about that fit is tested against the 5 mrad bound with propagated uncertainty (floor 1.16 mrad per reset). The fixed 0.9375 = 375/400 coefficient of v0.1–v0.4.2 is withdrawn. Non-targets with no target neighbour (8 or 9 per mask) are distance-2 controls. H2 Per reset, on at least 90% of directed pairs, in the target-|0⟩ arm: excess phase |Δφ| ≤ 5 mrad, excess loss of Bloch-vector length ≤ 2.5e-3, excess excitation ≤ 2e-4 (floors at 16,384 shots and r = 16: 0.69 mrad, 6.9e-4, 3.1e-5). The ζ-corrected target-|1⟩ excess phase is reported against the same 5 mrad bound as a consistency check. Refuted if fewer than 90% of pairs meet all three primary bounds, or the distance-2 controls show any excess beyond 3σ.
Q3. Crosstalk at scale: simultaneous against isolated resets Protocol, a frame-tracked reset cycle benchmark adapted from MCM cycle benchmarking (Hines and Proctor; Zhang et al.), whose classical register a reset lacks: m ∈ {1, 16, 64} cycles of [random Pauli Pq on every qubit → reset on the mask, delay(400 ns) elsewhere → Pq†], over the 118 qubits of Deviation 6; the three depths nest, m = 1 and m = 16 sharing the first cycles of the m = 64 frame (Deviation 7); the per-cycle idle seen by a spectator is 480 ns, the 400 ns reset or delay plus the two 40 ns Pauli layers. Reset qubits enter in |1⟩ and are read in Z; because reset erases history, only the final cycle's frame matters for them, and the expected outcome is 0 for P ∈ {I, Z} and 1 for P ∈ {X, Y}, recorded per circuit as expected_z. Without this tracking the twirled reset is I/2 and the Z readout is uninformative. The reset residual per qubit is ε = P(outcome ≠ expected) − readout error, a single-cycle quantity read at every m and required m-independent. Spectators enter in |+⟩ and are read in X; the frames twirl their error into a Pauli channel, and the per-cycle spectator error is the decay of ⟨X⟩ in m. Masks: the five sparse masks of Q2 (20%, isolated) and eight random 50% masks (dense), four Pauli frames each; each qubit is reset in one sparse mask and in about four dense masks. Static ZZ is not removed by the frame twirl, only converted into a Pauli channel: per cycle each idle neighbour pair moves weight sin²(ζτ/4) ≈ 4.1e-4 at the 27 kHz median over the 480 ns cycle idle (Paper 1 Deviation 34 convention; Deviation 7 corrects the 400 ns, 2.9e-4 figure of v0.4.3), common-mode between reset and idle neighbours at leading order, so the predicted dense − sparse residual from the Q2 ζ map is about 4e-5 (1.4e-4 at 50 kHz), at the 1.0e-4 floor and below the 1e-3 bound; it is predicted per qubit from the ζ map, reported beside H3, and a mid-cycle spectator echo, delay(200 ns) → X → delay(200 ns) on the spectators with the X folded into Pq† and the echo flag logged per circuit, is added to the cycle only if the predicted residual exceeds half the floor, 5e-5 (Deviations 5 and 7). H3 At fixed m, the reset residual under dense masks exceeds that under sparse masks by εdense − εsparse ≤ 1e-4, mean over the 111 qubits that are neither readout-flagged nor qubit 79 (floor 2.7e-5; the CZ-cluster flag is logged separately and does not exclude from this mean, Deviation 7); the per-cycle spectator error under dense masks exceeds that under sparse masks by ≤ 1e-3 at m = 64 (floor 1.0e-4). Refuted if the mean difference's 95% interval lies above 1e-4 at any m, or the spectator excess exceeds 1e-3 on more than 10% of qubits.
Q4. Reset-as-channel: does the pooled random-mask dial realise Np? Protocol: twelve two-qubit patches (one horizontal edge per row, by the rule in Section 3), run in parallel. For p ∈ {0.25, 0.5}, 16 stratified masks per p realise Np ⊗ Np exactly on every patch: the four reset patterns (Ra, Rb) ∈ {00, 10, 01, 11} appear 9/3/3/1 times at p = 0.25 and 4/4/4/4 times at p = 0.5, so the realised per-qubit p̂q equals p and the pattern floor on the pooled statistics is zero. (Random masks would not do: with K = 16 the pooled tz has a pattern s.d. of 0.108 at p = 0.25, above the bound, and Paper 1's K = 64 random masks carry a pattern s.d. of 0.054, which Q4 does not remove and Paper 1 must propagate.) Four inputs (|0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, |+i⟩) and three readout axes give the affine Bloch map r ↦ D r + t pooled over masks; the ⟨XX⟩ correlator on |+⟩|+⟩ tests for a correlated part. Intended: Np = (1 − p) I + p Reset, so D = (1 − p) I3, t = (0, 0, p). Every bound is tested against p and, as the general rule for any mask set, against the realised p̂q. H4 On every patch qubit: |tz − p̂q| < 0.05, |tx|, |ty| < 0.02, |Dii − (1 − p̂q)| < 0.05, off-diagonal |Dij| < 0.02, connected |⟨XX⟩ − ⟨X⟩⟨X⟩| < 0.02 (shot floors at 32,768 pooled shots: 5.5e-3 for t and ⟨XX⟩, 3.9e-3 for D). Refuted if any bound fails on more than one of the 24 qubits at either p; the dial is then not used in Paper 1 without a recorded deviation.
Q5. Time stability across the campaign Protocol: the native-reset subset of Q1 (eight circuits, 32,768 shots, init_qubits True) repeated on four further Paper 1 hardware days within 1–12 October 2026 (Deviation 3), each with that day's snapshot. H5 The device median native reset error changes by less than 2e-4 in absolute terms between any two days (floor 5.6e-5). Per-qubit changes are reported as a map and as the count of qubits moving by more than 1.5e-3 (3σ on a two-day difference at 32,768 shots), without a threshold: even the single-arm 3σ of 1.1e-3 is ten times the expected ε, so a per-qubit test would be vacuous. Refuted if the median drift's 95% interval lies above 2e-4 for any pair of days.

6Anomaly protocol

Mirrors Paper 1's Deviation 19. An anomaly is (i) a qubit or directed pair whose statistic deviates from its Gate P2-1 prediction by more than 3σ, σ combining shot floor and bootstrap interval, or (ii) a spatially correlated deviation: three or more mutually adjacent qubits, or a full row or column, deviating in the same direction by more than 2σ each. Confirmation requires re-measurement on a different calendar day, using at most 20 of the 60 reserve minutes shared with Paper 1, and failure of the calibrated simulation (snapshot T1, T2, readout and 1Q errors, fitted ZZ) to reproduce it within confidence intervals. An unreplicated anomaly is reported as exploratory and labelled so; a replicated one triggers a separate pre-registered follow-up before any claim beyond this paper.
