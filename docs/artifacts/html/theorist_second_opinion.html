Theorist's second opinion

First person, no softening; every arXiv id was fetched today.

Short version. The trainable-and-hard question is not open as the thread poses it, and the reset ansatz was never a candidate. I would co-author A with an n-ladder added and Theorem 8 demoted to motivation; not B without a tensor-network group. Paper 1 is sound but thin. Nature-family odds 1–2%; do not design for them.

1The open question, stated precisely

Status. Cerezo et al. (2312.09121) state no theorem. Two claims: architectures that provably avoid barren plateaus at random initialisation live in classically identifiable polynomial subspaces, and such subspaces are simulable, sometimes only "quantum-enhanced" after a data-acquisition phase on the device. They exclude warm starts and "very special parts of the landscape", and their SI has contrived non-concentrated, non-simulable losses built from cryptographic hardness.

Proven, simulable side. Angrisani et al. (2409.01706): noiseless locally scrambling random circuits, any observable, constant error in polynomial time, inverse-polynomial error in quasi-polynomial time, any geometry. Angrisani et al. (2501.13101): the same under any incoherent local noise, amplitude damping with γ up to 1 and constant-rate dynamic operations included; Appendix F says the average-case assumption is essential (quantum refrigerator). Mele et al. (2403.13927): non-unital noise gives Θ(1) local-cost variance and gradient norm, and the same paper gives an efficient estimator to constant accuracy with effective log depth. The most-cited escape is the cleanest instance of the dichotomy.

Dynamic circuits. Deshpande et al. (2411.05760) prove their DPQCs BP-free and concede Pauli-path simulability at random initialisation. Shirgure et al. (2606.23751) give the mechanism: anti-concentration from ancilla-supported Pauli terms while upstream parameters stay stranded (Lemmas 2–3); their "at least as hard as BP-free unitaries" is a purification argument, not a theorem. Zapusek et al. (2507.02043): 1/poly variance only within O(log n) layers of a reset.

Structure and warm starts. Ragone et al. (2309.09342) give the deep-circuit variance from the Lie algebra; Goh et al. (2308.01432) simulate exactly when it is polynomial. Puig et al. (2404.10044) guarantee gradients near a warm start; Lerch et al. (2411.19896) give every such patch a quantum-enhanced surrogate. Bermejo et al. (2408.12739): QCNNs are effectively simulable on the data they succeed on.

The other direction. Simulable does not imply trainable (deep depolarised Clifford circuits are simulable and flat). Trainable, non-dequantizable models exist by construction: Gil-Fuster et al. (2406.07072) give recipes; Anschuetz and Gao (2402.08606) prove polynomial memory separations for trainable QNNs. Both put the hardness in a fixed, non-variational block; neither touches a random hardware-efficient ansatz.

A proof needs trainable defined as Varθ[∂C] ≥ 1/poly over the whole parameter distribution, and a demonstration that this forces the second-moment weight of the Heisenberg-evolved observable onto a polynomial subspace for layers that are not 2-designs; known only for 2-designs. A counterexample needs 1/poly variance at random initialisation plus average-case hardness of estimating one local expectation to 1/poly accuracy, in the gap Pauli propagation leaves: constant versus inverse-polynomial accuracy in two dimensions, or non-2-design layers whose second moment does not contract. Local expectation values of random circuits are exactly what is easy; low odds from any hardware-efficient family.

Tractable project? Yes, not the conjecture. Six to nine months:

  1. Write the second-moment (Pauli-transfer) map of one dial layer for Ry/CZ with (a) the i.i.d. mixture reset and (b) a mask fixed per circuit.
  2. Diagonalise it on the light cone: α(p) and the variance floor for both; conjecture (1/3)p⁴ versus roughly (1/3)p² and a slower rate for (b).
  3. Treat the non-2-design correction: Ry leaves Y-strings unrotated, so Y-paths form a separate slow mode; decide whether Theorem 8's form survives.
  4. Pauli-propagation numerics to 60 qubits with truncation error, checked exactly to 12.
  5. Paper 2's hardware points as the test. Output: a Quantum/PRA paper on the trainability–simulability exchange rate under a non-unital dial; the theory half of Paper 2.

2Options A, B, C

I would co-author A. Not B: a noisy device is not a reference, so a hardware–classical residual is a noise-model statement, and Angrisani et al. already prove the average case; without a PEPS group the memo's own referee line is fatal. C collapses to A.

Changes I would insist on.

  • Add an n-ladder: n ∈ {40, 60, 100} at p = 0.25, L = 8, k = L. Corollaries 6 and 9 are statements about n; the grid has no n axis. Drop k = L−4 to pay for it.
  • Demote "test of Theorem 8". Ry/CZ is not a local 2-design (the pre-registration says so). The theorem supplies the form, not the constants; the pre-registered curve must be the second-moment calculation for this ansatz, drawn before data.
  • Make the truncation arm the headline. It measures Theorem 1 as an observable difference at 60–100 qubits with no ensemble variance; the redirect's resolved version is right.
  • Delete every sentence in which a Pauli-propagation residual "reopens the trainable-and-hard question". Disagreement with a truncated classical method is evidence of noise-model or truncation error, never of hardness.
  • Report p = 0.1 as exploratory; its floors sit at the shot floor.

Is the dial clean? On the estimand, yes after the reviewer's fix: by linearity the pooled-mask estimator targets Tr(PΦmix(ρ)). Not clean on three counts: the 2-design gap; the pattern-noise term Varmask[C]/K, to be subtracted like shot noise; and a reset compiled to measure-plus-X, whose noise is θ-independent only if the measurement is (run the rotation-survival identity at p > 0).

A PRX Quantum referee accepts α(p) monotone in log(1/c(p)) across three p, k = L variance flat in n, truncation RMS falling as cℓ/2, all pre-registered against the theorist's curve. Rejects one n, a k-profile only, residuals framed as hardness, or "a density-matrix simulation reproduces our curves".

3Paper 1

The Gate 1 reviewer is right: L‖t‖ ≈ 0.02 and a floor of 3×10⁻¹² make H3 untestable at native noise. Drop it; keep the k-sweep as an H2 check. Of the rest, H1 is a simulation claim, H2 is expected (Wang et al. 2007.14384; Mele Prop. 10), H4 is bookkeeping, though unreported at 100 qubits. I would sign it as a pre-registered hardware characterisation, QST or PRA class.

The single addition: the dial's n-ladder at L = 8 for p ∈ {0, 0.25}, same day, same patches. One figure: unital variance falling toward the floor with n beside the non-unital k = L curve flat in n, both against the second-moment prediction. That turns a decay catalogue into the hardware companion to Mele et al. Failing that, report εN and the count of resolvable parameters per point.

4The Nature question

No confirmation framing reaches referees at Nature Physics: the editor reads "hardware check of ref. 24", published there already. Nature Communications might send out "controllable non-unitality shows trainability and classical simulability rising together at 100 qubits" with a named theorist and a 100-qubit truncation figure: 20–30% to referees, 5% through, 1–2% overall. The only route up is a surprise, an α(p) or floor departing from second-moment theory and surviving noisy simulation; my first suspect would be the reset element. Design for a result you will defend, not for a journal.

5What the memos got wrong or missed

  • No n axis anywhere in Paper 2. A barren plateau is a statement about n.
  • Theorem 8 used as a quantitative form for an ansatz that is not a 2-design; the redirect never says so.
  • Both memos read a residual outside the CI as "reopening hardness": wrong direction of inference.
  • Neither cites Gil-Fuster et al. or Anschuetz–Gao: trainable, non-dequantizable models exist by construction, hardness in a non-variational block.
  • The killed memo's 2×W patches at n ≤ 12 concentrate toward 1/(2ⁿ+1) for every ansatz; they could never show a plateau or its absence. Mele et al.'s SI already has QAOA numerics, so "untested beyond 2-design" is inaccurate.
  • Pattern noise is quoted as a floor, not subtracted; and the dial budget presupposes Paper 1's unconfirmed budget. Fold the dial into Paper 1.
Overall. Run A as Paper 1's non-unital arm with the n-ladder and the truncation figure; give the theory to the five-line project; forget Nature; aim at PRX Quantum at an honest 20%, QST as the floor.
