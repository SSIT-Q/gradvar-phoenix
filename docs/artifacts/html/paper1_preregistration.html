1Hypotheses

Each hypothesis is a prediction about the variance, over random parameters, of one parameter-shift gradient of a local observable. The refutation condition is fixed here, before data.

H1. Noiseless structure For a two-qubit observable ZiZj on the square lattice, the noiseless gradient variance is independent of n at fixed depth L as long as the patch contains the causal cone of the observable (the radius-(L - 1) neighbourhood of the edge, about 2L2 qubits), so it is exactly n-independent at L = 1 and L = 2 on every patch of the ladder. Uvarov and Biamonte (2011.10530) give a lower bound 3-|C| for two-design blocks, which Ry/CZ blocks are not (their Table 1); on a 2D lattice it is polynomial in n only for L of order sqrt(log n) (Cerezo et al., 2001.00550, for the log-depth statement in 1D). Once the cone covers the patch (L of order 8 and above for n up to 100) and the circuit approaches a two-design, the variance decays exponentially in n. The noiseless prediction itself comes from simulation. Refuted if the noiseless simulation at L = 4, on patches of at least 7 by 7 (n at least 49; on the ladder the 8x10 and 10x10 patches), shows a variance that changes by more than a factor of 2 between n = 80 and n = 100 outside the bootstrap interval, or, as an exploratory part only (Deviations 37 and 43), if the measured L = 12 curve does not fit an exponential in n better than a power law; H1's confirmatory content is part 1, the n-scaling at L ≤ 8. L = 4 differences on the 4-row patches are boundary truncation and are not a test. The L = 1 and L = 2 comparisons are n-independent by construction and are reported as a pipeline check only.
H2. Noise, Pauli type Under gate noise modelled as local Pauli channels, the measured variance decays exponentially with depth L at fixed n, for every layer index k. The published bound of Wang et al. (2007.14384, Theorem 1) is sqrt(8 ln 2) NO ||ω||∞ ||η||1 sqrt(n) qcL+1 with c = 1/(2 ln 2) = 0.7213, a bound on the derivative itself for all parameters; at n = 100 and q = 1 - 2e-3 it is about 23 for every L up to 12, so it is uninformative for our ladder. The tighter analytic reference is Mele et al. (2403.13927, Proposition 10): Var[∂C] ≤ exp(-Ω(|P| + L)) under any unital non-unitary noise, with no n prefactor. The quantitative unital prediction curve is our own noisy simulation with per-edge CZ, single-qubit, dephasing and readout errors from the ibm_phoenix snapshot and the Bloch-translation vector set to zero (t = 0); the zeroth-order sanity model Var ∝ p2L for global depolarising noise is drawn beside it. Refuted if the variance at fixed n does not fall with L once the shot floor is subtracted over any evaluable series with both depths ≤ 8, or if the k = L points at two depths ≤ 8 sit at the same level within their intervals; the L = 8 / L = 12 pair is computed and reported but is exploratory (Deviations 37 and 43), and any evaluable series or pair failing refutes, all being listed (Deviation 42).
H3. Noise, non-unital Under any local non-unital noise acting after every layer, on average over circuits whose two-qubit gates form local 2-designs (Mele et al., 2403.13927, Nature Physics 22, 751, 2026, prove this for that ensemble; their SI numerics suggest it extends to structured ansätze), the variance of the derivative with respect to a light-cone parameter in layer k of an L-layer circuit is exp(-Θ(L - k)) (Theorem 8), and the variance of a local-observable cost is Θ(1) in n at any depth with proven lower bound (||t||2²/3)|P| (Corollary 6; theorem numbers follow the Nature Physics version, arXiv v3 Corollary 8; corrected from (1/3)||t||2|P| by Deviation 21), where t is the Bloch-translation vector of the per-layer noise channel (see also Singkanipa and Lidar, 2402.08721). On ibm_phoenix, with T1 about 178 μs and about 0.3 μs per layer, ||t||2 is about 1.7e-3 and the proven floor about 1e-12 for |P| = 2, below any shot floor, so the floor is not a hardware-testable prediction. The testable H3 signature is the dependence on the layer index k at fixed L, compared against a unital-only (t = 0) simulation of the same circuits and normalised by the noiseless dependence on (L - k) (Uvarov and Biamonte, Eq. 2), since the noiseless variance also falls with L - k. The discriminator is k = L versus k = 1. Refuted if, at fixed L in {8, 12}, the ratio Var[∂C](k = L) / Var[∂C](k = 1), corrected by the noiseless ratio from simulation, does not exceed the same ratio predicted by the unital-only noise model (t = 0) by more than the bootstrap interval. The asymptotic floor is below the shot floor and is not a test.
H4. Mitigation Readout mitigation (resilience level 1) rescales the estimated expectation values and therefore both the landscape variance and the shot variance by the same factor, about (1 - 2pro)-4 ≈ 1.07 at the snapshot readout error of 8.2e-3; it does not change the ratio of landscape variance to shot variance (Aghaei Saem et al., QST 11, 015049, 2026, Corollary 1). Zero-noise extrapolation (level 2) with coefficients c inflates the shot variance of the gradient estimator by ||c||12 at equal shots per circuit (Banchi, Branford and Waghela, QST 11, 035031, 2026, Eqs. 17 to 19): 4x for linear extrapolation from noise factors 1 and 3, 12.25x for Richardson from 1, 3, 5. It is expected to increase the variance of the gradient estimator at fixed total shots (Wang et al., 2109.01051); worst-case sample-complexity bounds (Quek et al., 2210.11505, Nature Physics 20, 1648, 2024) show that no mitigation scheme can restore exponentially small signals on scrambling circuits, but those bounds do not apply to a shallow local circuit and the authors note that local circuits may mitigate well. Level-2 variance inflation is predicted to grow with the number of gates in the observable's light cone (Quek et al. p. 1649; Kim et al. p. 503) and is reported against cone size. Kim et al. (Nature 618, 500, 2023) used 384,000 to 614,400 shots per ZNE-mitigated value; at our 4096 to 16384 shots per circuit the inflation is expected a priori, and the test is its size against ||c||12. Pre-registered extrapolator: linear in the noise factor from gains 1 and 3 (c = (3/2, -1/2), ||c||1 = 2). Fallback: if the gain-3 expectation value is within two standard errors of zero, the point is reported unmitigated and flagged; the extrapolator is never switched per point. Refuted if level 1 shifts the ratio of landscape variance to shot variance by more than its bootstrap interval at any main-grid point, or if the level-2 shot-variance inflation at n = 40 and 100, L = 2 and 8, differs from ||c||12 = 4 by more than its bootstrap interval, or does not grow with cone size from L = 2 to L = 8.
H2 and H3 make different predictions for the depth dependence and for the layer-index dependence: H2 predicts decay with L for every k; H3 predicts decay with L - k, so the last layer decays more slowly with L than earlier layers. At L ≤ 12 and Nighthawk T1 both hypotheses predict that all layers still decay, and the test is the k-dependence at fixed L. Noiselessly the variance also falls with L - k (Uvarov and Biamonte, Eq. 2 and Fig. 4), so the layer-index fit is made on the ratio of measured to noiseless-simulated variance; the discriminating statistic is the k = L series versus L, set against the k = 1 series (Section 3, comparison metric). The earlier draft's claim that these two theories contradict each other was wrong. They describe different noise channels, and ibm_phoenix has both. That claim is not part of this document.

3Analysis plan

  • Estimate. Variance of the gradient across the M draws, with a 95 percent bootstrap interval (10,000 resamples). The shot-noise contribution is subtracted and reported separately: measured variance = signal variance + shot variance, both reported.
  • Fits. Exponential in L at fixed n; exponential in n at fixed L; exponential in (L - k) for the layer-index sweep. Fit uncertainty from the bootstrap. A power law is fitted alongside each exponential and the better model is reported by AIC.
  • Mean as a null test. The sample mean of ∂C over the M draws is reported as a null test: for uniform draws on [0, 2π) it is exactly zero for any θ-independent noise (Arrasmith et al., p. 4 to 5, Lemma 1); a significant non-zero mean flags drift, parameter-dependent noise or a pipeline error. It is not a Yao and Hasegawa check.
  • Cost variance as a secondary estimand. Var[C] over the M draws from the unshifted circuits, and Var[C(θ+) - C(θ-)] from the shifted pair, are reported beside Var[∂C] on every panel. The Arrasmith et al. equivalence between plateaus and cost concentration holds up to a factor m²L², so this classifies exponential against polynomial but does not give the exponent. A single-parameter variance does not by itself establish presence or absence of a plateau; the distribution of ∂C over draws (max |∂C|, kurtosis) is also reported per point.
  • Resolvability ratio. The Aghaei Saem et al. ratio εN = Varshot / (N Varθ) is computed per point, required below 1 for any point to be claimed, and predicted in Gate 1.
  • Plots. All plots log scale, with the null-control floor and the analytic shot floor drawn on every panel.
  • Classical baselines beside every hardware point. Statevector to 24 qubits; MPS (Qiskit Aer) beyond; Pauli-propagation truncation sweep where a package is available. Noisy simulation uses the snapshot's per-edge CZ error, per-qubit 1Q error, T1 and T2, and readout confusion.
  • Comparison metric. Which of the unital-only (H2) and full non-unital (H3) simulations predicts the measured depth curve (k = 1) and the noiseless-normalised layer-index ratio Var[∂C](k = L) / Var[∂C](k = 1) within the bootstrap intervals. Both, one or neither is an acceptable outcome and is reported as such. The Deviation 14 ratio Rhardware / Runital is reported only where the k = 1 denominator lies above the shot floor; at native noise its predicted value is 0.98–1.05 on converged rows with a 2σ of 0.04–0.42, so it has no power and is a consistency check only (Deviation 40).

3bNon-unital arm: controlled reset dial

Deviation 16 reclassified H3 because the device's native non-unitality (||t|| ≈ 2e-3 per layer) puts every non-unital prediction below the shot floor. ibm_phoenix exposes a native reset on 119 qubits, and a reset applied to each qubit with probability p after each layer is a non-unital channel whose translation vector is set by p, not by T1. This arm turns that element into a dial and tests the non-unital predictions at strengths where they are resolvable. It runs on the same day and the same patches as the Paper 1 ladder points it is compared with, is booked as its own line in Section 6 (the 65-minute line for the booked core; the remaining points are contingent on unspent reserve or a granted 1 μs rep_delay), and is analysed with the Section 3 machinery unless stated otherwise. Every prediction below is a second-moment calculation for this Ry/CZ ansatz, drawn before data (Gate 1b). The arm is a variance-level benchmark of a first-principles noise model: the measured k = L gradient variance over the rigorous ansatz-specific floor of Deviation 33 (predicted 1.88 at p = 0.25 and 1.30 at p = 0.5 on the 53-qubit patch) is its headline statistic, because the floor is the part a reset creates trivially in the last layer and the excess is the model-dependent, deeper-path content. The ansatz is not a local 2-design, so Mele et al. (2403.13927) supply the functional forms, not the constants; nothing here is a test of their Theorem 8 (arXiv Theorem 10), which assumes 2-design layers, nor a demonstration of simulability. Theorem and corollary numbers follow the Nature Physics version (Corollary 6, Theorem 8); in arXiv v3 the same statements are Corollary 8 and Theorem 10.

Channel

A reset applied with probability p, averaged over the reset lottery, is the mixture channel Np(ρ) = (1 − p)ρ + p|0⟩⟨0| on each qubit after each layer. In Bloch form r → Dr + t with D = (1 − p)I and t = (0, 0, p), so the contraction parameter of Mele et al. is c = (||D||F² + ||t||²)/3 = (1 − p)² + p²/3: c = 0.583 at p = 0.25 and c = 0.333 at p = 0.5. The Corollary 6 lower bound on the cost variance is (||t||2²/3)|P|, for |P| = 2 (p²/3)² = p4/9: 4.3e-4 at p = 0.25 and 6.9e-3 at p = 0.5, against the 4096-shot floor of 2.4e-4 on C (1.8× and 28× the floor; the p = 0.25 floor comparison therefore rests on the pre-drawn ansatz curve, not on the bound). Because a reset layer follows the last Ry/CZ layer, Eθ[Cmix] = p² exactly for uniform θ before readout folding (ZiZj is 1 when both observable qubits are reset in the last layer and averages to zero otherwise; about 0.066 and 0.252 after folding through the readout confusion on the 53-qubit patch at p = 0.25 and 0.5), a free pipeline check reported beside the mean null test of Section 3. The floor tests use Varθ[Cmix], not E[Cmix²]; the non-zero mean is subtracted. The same channel is inside the HS-contractive class of Singkanipa and Lidar (2402.08721), whose noisy parameter-shift rule applies, and inside the noise class for which Angrisani et al. (2501.13101) prove average-case Pauli-propagation simulability; that is the point of the arm, not a caveat to it.

The arm is not run, and its minutes return to Question 1 repeats, if any of the following holds in the characterisation or dry run: (a) measured reset error above 2e-2 on any dial-patch qubit that cannot be swapped out within the clean component; (b) QPU-locked time above 7.0 minutes per dial gradient point, including job overhead, at the submitted rep_delay (the Deviation 27 budget of 5.95 min with 18 percent headroom, matching the 65-minute dial line; Deviation 41), the circuit-execution-only time being reported beside it and both extrapolated in the smoke test from its measured per-execution and per-job constants; on the 20 Sep smoke test's constants (3.0 s per job, Deviation 47) the Deviation 27 structure of 171 jobs put the point at 8.7 min (171 × 3.0 s alone is 8.6; evaluator 12.8 min), so the rule fired, and Deviation 48 packs each point's 512 mask pubs into 2 jobs, giving 0.29 min at L = 8 and 0.32 min at L = 12 at resilience 0 (0.93 min at resilience 1 with the 64-shot minimum), against which the 7.0-minute line, unchanged, is re-evaluated and passes with a factor above 7; (c) the transpiler maps reset to a measure-plus-conditional-X sequence (a measure count in the transpiled circuit above the terminal readout, or a dial layer above 1 μs from the target durations); (d) the Estimator primitive refuses a mid-circuit reset at the resilience level used on the Paper 1 grid. A Sampler fallback or a change of resilience level is not substituted without a recorded deviation. A failed rotation-survival identity at p > 0 on more than 1 of the 5 draws at a point flags that point as exploratory; it does not kill the arm.

Analysis

  • Pairing. Every ratio in H5 to H7 is formed on shared θ draws and, where the channel allows, shared mask seeds, and its interval is a paired bootstrap over draws (10,000 resamples) with a bootstrap over masks within draws for the pattern-noise term. Shot and pattern-noise floors are subtracted with their uncertainty propagated (Section 3, Estimate).
  • Fits. Exponential in L − k for the k = 1 series at each p; the flat model against a falling exponential for the k = L series in L and in n, compared by AIC; cℓ/2 against a free exponent for the truncation arm. The fitted exponent α(p) at p ∈ {0.25, 0.5} is reported against log(1/c(p)) with the unital references at p = 0.
  • Baseline beside every point. Pauli propagation with the mixture rule, exact to 12 qubits on the memo code, truncated with reported error beyond; the second-moment curves of Gate 1b; the noisy simulation of Section 3 for the controls.
  • Conclusion as pre-registered. The arm's conclusion is that a controlled non-unital channel raises the cost-variance floor and the late-layer gradient variance and, at the same time, makes the circuit forget its early layers, so that trainability and classical simulability rise together; average-case simulability of this noise class is proven (Angrisani et al., 2501.13101). Simulability is stated as the conclusion of the paper's non-unital section, not hidden in a caveat. A residual between hardware and the truncated classical baseline is reported as a noise-model or truncation discrepancy under the Deviation 19 anomaly protocol; it is not read as evidence of hardness.
  • Reporting. All dial panels carry the shot floor, the pattern-noise floor, the pre-drawn second-moment curve with its truncation band, and the matched controls. Points designated exploratory by the rotation-survival flag or by Gate 1b's inconclusive branch are labelled as such.
