# Paper 1 upside: PRX Quantum odds and the options for a stronger result

22 September 2026, version 2 (all fourteen corrections of the independent review applied; review record `review_paper1_upside_memo_2026-09-22.md`). Nothing here is booked, armed or adopted. Sources: repository `main` at 369af38 (Paper 1 pre-registration mirror v0.15.1, `docs/HANDOVER.md` Version 9, `docs/HANDOVER_MEMORY.md`, decision memo of 19 Sep, reserve proposal v2 of 21 Sep) and the papers in Section 8, re-checked on 22 Sep 2026 (arXiv API, OpenAlex, Crossref).

## 1. Bottom line

1. **PRX Quantum odds for Paper 1 as scoped: about 5 %** (my judgement, range 3 to 10 %). Eventual acceptance at one of QST, npj Quantum Information or PRA: about 60 to 70 % once the campaign completes cleanly. Nature family: below 1 %. The earlier documents gave Paper 1 only qualitatively ("QST/npj QI by default, PRX Quantum only if the data contradict a theorem"; Nature family 1 to 2 % per direction, handover Section 1); the reserve proposal's percentages refer to the Section 3c options, not to Paper 1 as scoped. The 5 % and 60 to 70 % figures are new judgements consistent with that framing; the Nature figure is lower than the handover's, for the reasons in item 2.
2. **Why not higher.** The hypotheses test established theory whose expected outcome is confirmation: H1 to H3, and H5 to H7, which test Mele et al. (Nature Physics 22, 751, 2026), a paper that also gives an efficient classical algorithm for most circuits in the same noisy regime. Schmitt et al. (arXiv 2602.22851, February 2026) already report the absence of noise-induced barren plateaus on 8 to 102 IBM qubits and attribute it to T1-dominated non-unital noise. The qualitative non-unital conclusion is therefore not new. What Paper 1 adds is a controlled manipulation of the non-unital strength, parameter-shift variances with confidence intervals against a measured null floor, and pre-registered quantitative tests.
3. **No realistic breakthrough route exists inside this programme before the mid-November arXiv target** (my judgement; the Flex plan runs to January 2027). A breakthrough here would be a regime that is trainable and demonstrably not classically simulable at about 100 qubits. The results on such regimes are theoretical constructions (Section 3), and the one recent direction its authors frame as a route to practical advantage (phase transitions in parametrized circuits) has no gradient content and would be a separate paper, which Owais ruled out on 21 Sep.
4. **Minutes are not the constraint.** 315.33 of 360 Flex minutes remain and the booked programme needs far fewer (Section 5). The pre-registration's surplus rule (Section 6) spends unspent minutes first on Deviation 17's larger M on the main grid, second on restoring the Section 3b cut-order items, third on the reserve, and "never to new arms without a Deviation".
5. **Recommended, in order:** (a) run the queued lists as pre-registered, and restore the contingent dial items, ℓ = 4 of H7 among them (about 1.9 min under budget model v3), through the surplus rule's second tier, with no Deviation needed; (b) start the per-draw classical surrogate now (no QPU minutes; it unlocks Section 3c and the classical-simulability baseline for the n = 85 main-grid points); (c) add a second dial strength to H7 (Option B) only if its Deviation can be adopted before post-run review 06 reads H5 and H7; its gain is modest; (d) frame the paper around what Schmitt et al. do not have.

## 2. Where Paper 1 stands

- **Design.** Hardware-efficient ansatz (Ry and CZ) on rectangular patches of ibm_phoenix (Nighthawk r2, 120 qubits), ladder n about 20 to 100, depths 1 to 12, parameter-shift gradient variance over M = 200 draws; H1 to H4 on the main grid; the reset-dial non-unital arm H5 to H7 (Section 3b, Deviation 20, Gate 1b); Section 3c surrogate tracking at n = 85 (Deviation 56: Block C booked, blocks A to F conditional on a certified per-draw surrogate).
- **Results so far** (from the handover digest of post-run reviews 03 and 04): variance flat in n from 19 to 85 at every depth (H1 supported, no 2^-n plateau); fall with depth 8 of 8 (H2); points at L <= 4 inside their prediction intervals (ratios 0.80 to 1.26); hardware tracks noiseless gradients draw by draw (attenuation 0.85 to 0.92 at L <= 2); ZNE recovers none of the L = 2 attenuation and doubles the L = 8 variance; two firm Deviation 19 flags (n = 19, L = 4 at 0.61x; n = 85, L = 8 at 0.50x the run-day prediction, z = -5.1), queued for replication with fresh seeds.
- **Not yet run:** campaign day 3 (dial arm and Gate 1b references), replication 01 (two lists), Section 3c Block C, the L2-c5 resubmission, `grid_n40_repeat`. Paper 2 hardware waits on the PI's signature on its Deviation 9.

## 3. What the literature allows

| Result | Consequence for Paper 1 |
|---|---|
| Mele et al., Nature Physics 22, 751 (2026), arXiv 2403.13927: any noise truncates most circuits to effectively logarithmic depth for expectation values; under non-unital noise local costs have no barren plateau; the same effective shallowness yields an efficient classical algorithm, with high probability over the circuit. | H5 to H7 test a theorem whose outcome is expected. A controlled hardware test is useful, but the regime is classically simulable on average by the same paper, so it cannot carry an advantage claim. |
| Cerezo et al., Nature Communications 16, 7907 (2025), arXiv 2312.09121: case-by-case evidence that structures which avoid barren plateaus admit classical simulation, given an initial data-acquisition phase on a quantum device (a perspective, not a theorem). | Referees will ask for a classical baseline beside each hardware claim. Section 3c's surrogate is that baseline for the n = 85 main-grid points; the dial arm's baseline is the pre-drawn average-case curves. |
| Schmitt et al., arXiv 2602.22851 (Feb 2026): absence of noise-induced barren plateaus on IBM hardware, 8 to 102 qubits (mostly Eagle, per the decision memo), from information-content landscape analysis, an estimator of gradient norms rather than parameter shift. They attribute the effect to T1-dominated non-unital noise (with 8-qubit simulations) and see saturation beyond a characteristic runtime of hundreds of microseconds, far beyond Paper 1's L <= 12. They use a null model without an interval. | Removes the qualitative headline, not only a "first at 100 qubits" claim. What stays ours: parameter-shift variance with confidence intervals against a measured null floor; a controlled non-unital dial (a manipulation, not an observation); pre-registered quantitative predictions; layer-index structure (H6); the effective-depth test (H7); the mitigation-level surface. Gradient data on a Nighthawk device: none found in the 19 Sep search; any "first" wording is the theorist's call at submission. |
| Angrisani et al., PRX Quantum 7, 020313 (2026), Pauli propagation under arbitrary local noise; Fontana et al., npj Quantum Information 11, 84 (2025), classical simulation of noisy variational circuits. | Classical tools for the noisy and non-unital regime exist and are improving. A per-draw surrogate is buildable; a claim of classical hardness in this regime would not survive review. |
| Trainable-yet-not-simulable theory: Anschuetz and Gao, Quantum 10, 1976 (2026), arXiv 2402.08606 (provable polynomial memory separations for constructed trainable models); Gil-Fuster et al., arXiv 2406.07072 (when trainability and non-dequantization can coexist); Xinbiao Wang et al., arXiv 2608.02325 (Aug 2026; a resource theory of learnability beyond classical simulation). | Constructed models, definitions and resource theories. In my judgement none supplies a protocol for a 120-qubit device before the November target. |
| Xiaoyang Wang et al., arXiv 2603.27532 (Mar 2026): parametrized circuits with phase transitions, with heuristic evidence that tensor-network and Pauli-propagation simulation become less efficient near criticality; framed by its authors as a route towards practical advantage. | No trainability or gradient content, so it does not bear on Paper 1's hypotheses. It is the closest thing to a breakthrough direction in the current literature, and it would be a different paper. |
